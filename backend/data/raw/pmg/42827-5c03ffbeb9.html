Mkhwanazi Inquiry: Correspondence and reports

Ad Hoc Committee to Investigate Allegations made by Lieutenant General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi

28 April 2026
Chairperson: Mr M Lekganyane (ANC)
Share this page:

Meeting Summary

Video

Ad Hoc Committee on Gen Mkhwanazi's Allegations

Confidential Documents:

  • Presentation: Preliminary Report Structure
  • IGI Report
  • Correspondence from Busisiwe Mkhwebane Foundation
  • Correspondence from Ms Sarah Jane Trent’s Attorneys

The Ad Hoc Committee met to discuss draft reports from evidence leaders and content advisors, correspondence from the Busisiwe Mkhwebane Foundation and Ms Sarah Jane Trent's attorneys, and arrangements for viewing the Inspector-General of Intelligence report.

The Committee expressed concerns over delays in finalising the evidence leaders' report, attributing them to insufficient resources, incomplete transcripts, and the substantial volume of evidence requiring integration under thematic areas aligned with its terms of reference. It noted that only one transcript remained outstanding, expected by week's end, and supported a submission deadline of 8 May 2026, with Members' inputs due by 15 May to allow incorporation into a consolidated draft. The Committee raised apprehensions about resource constraints hindering progress, requesting additional support such as re-engaging Advocate Lerato Zikalala and urging the Speaker for enhanced administrative assistance to avoid further extensions.

Regarding correspondence, the Committee voiced unease with the Busisiwe Mkhwebane Foundation's claims of a promised in-camera hearing for Mr Philasande Dotyeni on allegations surrounding Lieutenant General Sindile Mfazi's death, clarifying no formal decision had been made and emphasising the need for legal guidance on handling classified information. It questioned the handling of Ms Sarah Jane Trent's request to expunge her testimony based on a confidential psychological report, rejecting reconsideration as the National Assembly Rules do not permit such actions and suggesting court recourse instead, while criticising the informal submission of sensitive medical records. Concerns were also highlighted about the Inspector-General of Intelligence report's return by the Speaker due to communication gaps, prompting calls for direct engagement to enable secure viewing per protocol.

The Committee agreed to seek an extension of its lifespan to late May or early June to accommodate deliberations and report finalisation, stressing adherence to timelines to maintain public confidence and procedural integrity. It reiterated the importance of formal evidence submission over public or social media sources to uphold the process's credibility. Resolutions included the Chairperson contacting the Speaker on the intelligence report and formal responses to the correspondence, ensuring all matters align with parliamentary rules.

The meeting concluded with the Committee extending condolences to a Member and his family on the passing of his mother, with multiple Members offering sympathy, prayers, and support, which the Member acknowledged as a comfort during the grieving process.

The Chairperson subsequently determined that the meeting did have a quorum.

Adv G Breytenbach (DA) stated that she had joined the meeting but was uncertain how long she would be able to remain connected, as she was in Istanbul. She further tendered an apology on behalf of Ms D Kohler Barnard (DA).

Ms K Sangoni (ANC) conveyed an apology from Mr M Ntuli (ANC), indicating that he would join the meeting late. She added that she herself was on oversight in Limpopo and that her network connection might be unstable.

Mr Skosana indicated that Mr S Nomvalo (MKP) and Mr C Shongwe (MKP) were still engaged in organisational work.

Chairperson’s Opening Remarks

The Chairperson informed Members that reports had been received from the Committee Secretariat, as well as a further report from the evidence leaders approximately 40 to 45 minutes prior to the meeting. He indicated uncertainty as to whether the evidence leaders had joined the meeting. He stated that the Committee would consider these reports and that he would propose a way forward regarding their handling.

He further advised that there were two additional items on the agenda: correspondence from the Advocate Busisiwe Mkhwebane Foundation and correspondence from the legal representatives of Ms Sarah Jane-Trent. He indicated that the Parliamentary legal team, led by Mr Tetyana, would present on these matters and provide a proposal regarding how the Committee would consider the report promised by the Minister.

The Chairperson expressed his appreciation to Members for making themselves available, noting that they had had sufficient time to rest and re-energise. He acknowledged that many Members were engaged in oversight and constituency work and, for that reason, indicated that the meeting would not be unduly prolonged.

He extended his gratitude to Ms Nicolette van Zyl-Gous and her team for their work in ensuring that the reports were made available, noting that the work had been completed under considerable pressure ahead of the 30 April deadline. He further thanked Mr Tetyana for the legal support provided to the Committee, as well as the Committee’s support staff for their coordination and planning. He also expressed appreciation to the Office of the House Chairperson, Mr Frolick, and the Office of the Speaker for their support.

The Chairperson informed Members that the draft report would be formally launched during the meeting. He proposed that Members be afforded an opportunity to indicate the time required to study the report and to make submissions. He explained that, once Ms van Zyl-Gous had received Members’ inputs, she would incorporate them into a final draft, which would be presented to the Committee on an agreed date.

He further indicated that Ms van Zyl-Gous would first take Members through the key highlights of the report to guide their review.

Regarding the legal team’s report, the Chairperson noted that, although a submission had been received, the report was not yet complete. He had anticipated that the legal team would indicate the additional time required to finalise the report, after which the Committee could decide on any proposals arising therefrom. He emphasised that the report remained incomplete.

In respect of the Minister’s report, he stated that Members would be requested to propose a suitable time for the Committee to view it.

With regard to the two pieces of correspondence received, the Chairperson indicated that the legal team would present on the matters and advise the Committee on how they should be addressed.

Finally, he noted that the Speaker’s letter had set a deadline of 30 April. As the Committee was meeting on that date, he proposed that the Committee consider writing back to the Speaker. He explained that this item would be addressed last, as the Committee would first need to deliberate on timeframes before advising the Speaker on when it expected to finalise its report.

Adoption of the Agenda

The Chairperson invited Members to propose any additional items for inclusion in the agenda.

There was no response from Members.

The Chairperson observed that there were no indications from Members and assumed that the Committee agreed to the agenda. He further referred to his proposal regarding the formal launch of the report and invited Members to indicate if they held a different view.

Mr Skosana stated that he had missed a significant portion of the discussion due to network difficulties and requested that the Chairperson summarise the proposal.

The Chairperson explained that the proposal was for the Committee to formally launch the reports during the meeting, after which Members would indicate the time required to study the reports and make submissions to Ms van Zyl-Gous. He added that, once Members’ inputs had been received, these would be incorporated into a final draft, which the Committee would then consider on an agreed date. He enquired whether the proposal was clear.

Mr Skosana confirmed that the proposal was clear and expressed his support.

The Chairperson further noted that, regarding the legal team’s report, a submission had been made but remained incomplete. He observed that Adv Arendse was present and indicated that the legal team would be requested to inform the Committee of the additional time required to finalise the report.

Engagement with Evidence Leaders

Adv Norman Arendse, Senior Counsel, apologised for joining the meeting late, explaining that he had been waiting to be admitted and that some of his colleagues were unable to attend as they were occupied with other work commitments. He expressed his appreciation for being able to participate in the meeting.

Adv Arendse stated that, through the Chairperson’s personal assistant, the Committee had requested an indication a few days earlier as to when a report, or at least a draft report, would be submitted for consideration. He explained that he had delayed responding due to the intensive work required to compile the report.

He informed the Committee that the evidence leaders had been working under significant logistical constraints. They had reviewed most of the transcripts, statements, and attached exhibits of key witnesses, to the extent that these had been received. However, he noted that certain transcripts, including those of Mr O’Sullivan and Mr McBride, had not yet been received. He emphasised that these transcripts were important for verifying the evidence, particularly in relation to questions posed by Committee Members and the responses provided by witnesses, which were not always reflected in written statements and exhibits.

Adv Arendse reported that an incomplete draft had been compiled and submitted earlier that afternoon to Mr Tetyana and Mr Ramaano. He explained that the draft primarily addressed the evidence of Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi, who had been the first witness over two days of proceedings in the previous year. The draft further covered issues relating to alleged political interference, the Political Killings Task Team (PKTT), procurement irregularities, and the evidence of General Dimpane, the Acting National Commissioner, as well as General Fani.

He indicated that, although substantial summaries of the evidence had been prepared, these alone would amount to a document exceeding 400 to 500 pages. However, he emphasised that the more critical task was to align and integrate these summaries under thematic areas corresponding with the Committee’s terms of reference. This process required careful organisation, integration, and consultation among the evidence leaders, which had been time-consuming. He apologised for any inconvenience caused by the delay.

Adv Arendse advised that, subject to the receipt of outstanding transcripts, which he indicated were being received rapidly and were approaching approximately 90% completion, a draft report could be provided within a week.

The Chairperson indicated that he had not clearly heard the timeframe for the submission of the final draft and requested clarification.

Adv Arendse clarified that the draft report would be ready within a week, taking into account the intervening public holidays and long weekends. He suggested that the report would likely be ready by the end of the following week, noting that this timeline aligned with an earlier proposal made by Mr Tetyana on behalf of the legal team.

The Chairperson enquired whether the evidence leaders would also be able to present an overview of the report once the final draft had been submitted.

Adv Arendse confirmed that, upon submission of the final draft, the evidence leaders would be available to present an overview. He added that Members would require sufficient time, potentially several days or up to a week, to consider the report in detail, particularly given the substantial nature of both the evidence leaders’ report and the report prepared by Ms van Zyl-Gous and her team. He further noted that the two reports would ultimately need to be integrated into a single comprehensive document.

He suggested that the Committee allocate time to review the report and provide comments, after which the evidence leaders could present the report, potentially by the end of the following week.

The Chairperson proposed that, once the final draft was ready, it should be formally received in a Committee meeting. He suggested that, similar to the current process with Ms van Zyl-Gous, the evidence leaders should provide a presentation highlighting the key aspects of the report to guide Members in their review.

Adv Arendse agreed with this approach, noting that it would assist in guiding Members through what would be a detailed and heavily referenced document. He emphasised that the report would contain extensive footnotes referencing sworn statements, oral evidence, and exhibits, which were essential to maintaining the integrity and accuracy of the report. He added that the process of cross-referencing was time-intensive but necessary to ensure that the final document, as part of the parliamentary record, would be a fair and accurate reflection of the Committee’s deliberations.

Presentation: Preliminary Report Structure

The Chairperson thanked Adv Arendse and indicated that Members had taken note of his input. He stated that Ms van Zyl-Gous would now take the Committee through the highlights of the draft report that had been circulated, after which discussions would follow.

Ms Nicolette van Zyl-Gous, Content Advisor, indicated that Ms Christine Silkstone had been leading the drafting of the report and accordingly handed over to her to present the salient aspects.

Ms Christine Silkstone, Content Advisor, led the presentation on the draft report. She outlined the context, the structure of the draft report, the proposed structure for Part A, the proposed structure for Parts B and C, and additional notes.

The Chairperson referred to two documents that had been circulated, noting that one appeared to assist Members in understanding the proceedings and how the presentations interacted with the evidence. He enquired whether Ms Silkstone’s presentation encompassed both documents.

Ms Silkstone explained that the second document referred to a discussion document prepared by the researchers and Ms van Zyl-Gous. She clarified that this document was intended to guide the Committee’s deliberations and would ultimately inform the final report. However, she noted that it did not form part of the formal report at this stage and was not presented as findings, but rather served as a briefing or guidance document.

The Chairperson affirmed that this clarification was important for Members to understand that only one formal report was being presented, supported by a set of briefing notes or guiding documents to assist in deliberations. He stated that Members could refer to these notes when engaging with the report.

He thanked Adv Arendse, Ms van Zyl-Gous, and Ms Silkstone for their presentations. He then invited Members to express their views.

The Chairperson noted that Adv Arendse had requested a week to finalise the report and sought clarity on whether this referred to 8 May or the following Wednesday. He requested Adv Arendse to specify the exact date on which the draft would be submitted.

Adv Arendse responded that, following consultation with his colleagues, 8 May would be the appropriate date for submission.

The Chairperson confirmed that 8 May would be the expected date for the final draft. He indicated that, once the draft had been received, the Committee would convene a meeting for the presentation of an overview.

He further noted that Ms van Zyl-Gous and Ms Silkstone had already presented on the draft structure and suggested that Members should indicate the time they required to consider the reports. He proposed that the Committee also agree on a deadline for the submission of Members’ inputs, so that these could be incorporated into a consolidated final draft for consideration at the next meeting.

The Chairperson then opened the floor for deliberations by Members.

Agreement on Timeframes and Support for Evidence Leaders

Response on Support Required

Adv Arendse referred to a request he had made at a previous meeting, which he recalled had been supported by Ms Sangoni. He proposed that the services of Adv Lerato Zikalala be secured. He explained that Adv Zikalala had previously been part of the team until the end of the previous year, including during proceedings at the Kgosi Mampuru Correctional Facility, and was therefore familiar with the evidence and key witnesses, including Mr Matlala.

Adv Arendse stated that formalising this arrangement, through an instruction issued by the Speaker’s Office via Mr Tetyana to the State Attorney, would significantly assist the team. He added that engaging a new advocate at this stage would be impractical, as it would require additional time for familiarisation with the evidence. He emphasised that the support required was no longer of a forensic or investigative nature, but rather assistance in finalising the report. He concluded that Adv Zikalala would be ideally suited to provide such support.

Mr Andile Tetyana, Parliament Legal Advisor, reiterated that Counsel’s client was the Committee and that the legal team would act in accordance with the Committee’s instructions. He indicated that, should the Committee support the proposal to engage Adv Zikalala, the necessary administrative processes would need to be followed.

He outlined that this would include submitting a memorandum to the Speaker to obtain political authorisation, followed by a memorandum to the Secretary to Parliament for financial authorisation, and thereafter engaging the State Attorney. He added that, assuming Adv Zikalala’s availability, the process could proceed accordingly, although it would likely only be finalised in the following week due to the shortened week.

The Chairperson invited Ms Mathys to comment.

There was no response from Ms Mathys.

The Chairperson indicated that the matter would be processed accordingly and requested Mr Tetyana to proceed with the necessary steps outside the meeting.

Request for Extension

Mr Tetyana raised a further matter, noting that the issue of an extension had been discussed. He cautioned that, if not formally addressed, the Committee’s lifespan would lapse at the end of the week, after which it could only be revived by the House. He therefore requested clarity on the Committee’s instruction regarding a formal request for an extension.

The Chairperson responded that the matter had been scheduled as the final item on the agenda, but noted that Members had already expressed support for an extension until the end of May or the first or second week of June. He enquired whether this provided sufficient guidance for the preparation of the submission.

Mr Tetyana sought clarification as to whether this instruction related to the request for an extension or to the processes concerning political and financial authorisations.

The Chairperson clarified that his reference was specifically to the request for an extension.

Mr Tetyana confirmed that the legal team would proceed in accordance with the Committee’s direction, whether the extension was to the end of May or June.

The Chairperson indicated that a submission would be prepared reflecting the various possible timelines. He noted that, although the Committee had not yet commenced its formal deliberations on the report, it was anticipated that this would begin once the reports were available. He suggested that the Committee’s work would likely be concluded around the first or second week of June.

The Chairperson then invited Mr Tetyana to proceed with his presentation on the remaining items.

Inspector-General of Intelligence (IGI) Report

Mr Tetyana reminded the Committee that there had been ongoing correspondence with the Minister regarding the IGI report. He stated that the Minister had not, in principle, opposed the Committee’s viewing of the report, provided that this was done in accordance with the National Assembly’s protocol on the handling of classified information.

He informed the Committee that, earlier that day, correspondence had been addressed to Mr Masibulele Xaso, Secretary to the National Assembly, seeking clarity on logistical arrangements for the viewing of the report. These included the proposed date, venue, and whether the necessary security arrangements had been made with the relevant authorities, including the South African Police Service.

Mr Tetyana reported that a response had been received at 16:57, shortly before the commencement of the meeting. He explained that the Committee had approached Mr Xaso on the basis that the report had already been submitted to Parliament by the Minister and subsequently referred to the Speaker, in line with the protocol, which vests custodianship of classified information in the Speaker of the National Assembly.

However, according to Mr Xaso’s response, the Speaker had returned the report to the Minister. Mr Tetyana indicated that this appeared to have been due to a lack of communication between the Chairperson and the Speaker. He therefore urged that the Chairperson engage directly with the Speaker to resolve any miscommunication. He added that such engagement might also involve the Acting Minister.

Mr Tetyana concluded that, as matters stood, the Committee was not yet in a position to confirm arrangements for the viewing of the IGI report.

Correspondence from the Busisiwe Mkhwebane Foundation

Mr Tetyana proceeded to address correspondence received from the Busisiwe Mkhwebane Foundation, dated 1 April 2026. He noted that the correspondence related to evidence presented by Mr Philasande Dotyeni, who had appeared before the Committee on 3 February 2026.

He explained that the Foundation’s submission centred on allegations that Lieutenant General Sindile Mfazi had been poisoned by individuals within the South African Police Service, and that his death had not resulted from a COVID-19-related condition, contrary to prevailing accounts. These allegations, he noted, had been conveyed to the Committee by Mr Dotyeni.

Mr Tetyana further stated that the Foundation contended that the Committee had undertaken to receive in camera evidence from Mr Dotyeni, specifically relating to the alleged murder of Lieutenant General Mfazi and the purported suppression of investigative files. He indicated that the correspondence effectively placed the Committee on terms, as reflected in paragraph seven of the letter.

He advised that the Committee would need to determine how to proceed, noting that Rule 167(f) of the National Assembly Rules empowered committees to determine their own working arrangements. He added that the Foundation appeared to be requesting that the Committee facilitate the previously contemplated in camera session with Mr Dotyeni.

Correspondence from the Attorneys of Ms Sarah Jane Trent

Mr Tetyana then addressed correspondence from the attorneys of Ms Sarah-Jane Trent. He recalled that Ms Trent had written to the Committee on 23 April 2026, requesting that her evidence, presented on 5 March 2026, be declared pro non scripto and expunged from the evidentiary record.

He noted that, in her earlier correspondence, Ms Trent had indicated that she was awaiting a forensic psychological evaluation report. He informed the Committee that this report had since been submitted and was of a confidential nature.

Mr Tetyana explained that the Rules of the National Assembly permitted a Committee, in terms of Rule 186, to exclude members of the public and the media from its proceedings. He suggested that the Committee could rely on this provision to consider the report in a closed session. He added that the Committee should deliberate on the matter after having seen the report.

Discussion on the Handling of the Report

The Chairperson requested clarity on the legal team’s recommendation regarding the last item.

Mr Tetyana reiterated that, given the sensitive nature of the report and its implications for Ms Trent’s privacy rights, it should be considered in a closed, in-person meeting. He further suggested that the process should mirror the procedures outlined in the protocol governing the handling of classified information. He added that, following consideration of the report, the Committee could determine the request.

The Chairperson enquired as to the current location of the report.

Mr Tetyana responded that the report had been sent, under strict conditions, to the Chairperson, Adv Arendse, and Mr Ramaano. He indicated that the covering letter accompanying the report specified the conditions under which it was to be handled.

The Chairperson raised concerns regarding this approach. He questioned why a report deemed confidential had been distributed to Members, rather than being presented directly to the Committee under controlled conditions. He expressed the view that, if the report were to be leaked, responsibility might unfairly be attributed to the Committee, despite the report having been circulated by its authors.

Mr Tetyana responded by outlining the standard procedures typically followed in handling confidential information, such as those applicable to the IGI report. He explained that such processes usually involved controlled access in a secure venue, with strict measures in place, including the collection of electronic devices, attendance registers, and, where necessary, the destruction of documents after viewing. These measures, he noted, were intended to minimise the risk of unauthorised disclosure.

The Chairperson maintained that Parliament, as an institution, could not be subjected to conditions imposed unilaterally by private individuals. He argued that, if a party wished to present confidential information to the Committee, it should assume responsibility for its safeguarding and formally request a meeting to present the report under appropriate conditions. He expressed concern that accepting such conditions could expose the Committee to undue liability in the event of a breach.

He concluded by emphasising that, from a legal perspective, it was inappropriate for external parties to impose obligations on Parliament regarding the handling of their own documents. He reiterated that a more appropriate approach would have been for the authors to request a formal meeting to present the report themselves.

Mr Tetyana reiterated that, in terms of the applicable protocol, classified information of this nature was required to be submitted to the Speaker, which had indeed occurred. He explained that the Speaker had subsequently returned the report to the Minister. He added that, according to correspondence from Mr Xaso, this may have been due to a lack of communication between the Chairperson, the Speaker, and, potentially, the Chairperson of the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence.

The Chairperson sought clarity on who should initiate the necessary correspondence and enquired whether it should be the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence.

Mr Tetyana responded that, based on Mr Xaso’s communication, the issue appeared to relate primarily to a lack of direct engagement between the Chairperson and the Speaker. He stated that, while the Committee had engaged with the Minister and secured his agreement in principle to make the report available, subject to compliance with the protocol, the report had been returned due to this apparent communication gap. He therefore advised that the matter be addressed directly by the Chairperson.

The Chairperson noted that the Committee had already informed the Minister of its decision.

Mr Tetyana confirmed that this was correct.

The Chairperson then enquired whether the report had been accompanied by a covering letter when it was submitted to the Speaker.

Mr Tetyana indicated that he was not in a position to confirm this.

The Chairperson proposed that a formal letter be addressed to the Speaker to communicate the Committee’s decision.

Mr Tetyana advised that the Chairperson should engage directly with the Speaker on the matter.

The Chairperson agreed with this approach.

Members’ Inputs on the Three Matters

The Chairperson indicated that he would proceed to take inputs from Members on the three matters under consideration.

ANC

Ms Sangoni expressed support for the proposal that the Chairperson engage with the Speaker regarding the IGI report. She indicated that there appeared to be some confusion regarding the circumstances under which the report had been returned. She suggested that it was possible that the Speaker had not been fully apprised of the purpose for which the report had been submitted.

Ms Sangoni stated that, in her understanding, the Minister had acted in accordance with the Committee’s request by making the report available, subject to compliance with the applicable protocol. She further noted that she was uncertain as to the role of the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence in this matter, as she did not recall any specific provision in the protocol assigning it a role in such circumstances. She suggested that clarity be sought from the Speaker and that a request be made for the report to be resubmitted so that the Committee could view it in accordance with the protocol.

Turning to the matter concerning Ms Sarah-Jane Trent, Ms Sangoni indicated that she agreed with the Chairperson’s concerns regarding the handling of the report. She expressed particular concern as to why the Committee had been provided with Ms Trent’s medical records. She recalled that the Committee had previously considered Ms Trent’s request and had concluded that the Rules of the National Assembly did not provide for the expungement of evidence already given before a Committee. She added that the Committee had further agreed that Ms Trent’s explanation of her mental state could be treated as supplementary to her evidence.

Ms Sangoni questioned the purpose of the medical records, noting that the Committee had not sought to assess Ms Trent’s bona fides and that such matters did not fall within the Committee’s mandate or expertise. She stated that, in her view, the records did not alter the applicable legal position, as there remained no rule permitting the expungement of sworn testimony. She further recalled that the Committee had previously indicated that Ms Trent was at liberty to seek relief from a court, should she wish to pursue the matter.

She therefore stated that the ANC did not support any proposal for the Committee to consider the medical records, irrespective of the manner in which they were to be handled, as no value would be derived from doing so.

With regard to the correspondence concerning Mr Dotyeni, Ms Sangoni stated that the ANC was concerned by the tone of the letter from the Busisiwe Mkhwebane Foundation, which appeared to suggest that the Committee was engaged in a cover-up by failing to afford Mr Dotyeni an in-camera hearing.

She recalled that, during Mr Dotyeni’s appearance, he had indicated that there were matters he could not address in an open forum. In response, she had enquired whether he wished to present such matters in camera. She further recalled that the Chairperson had asked whether the information in question would be classified, to which Mr Dotyeni had responded in the affirmative.

Ms Sangoni stated that the Committee had then requested legal advice on how to handle classified information within the constraints of its mandate, particularly given the challenges associated with intelligence-related material. She indicated that, to her knowledge, this advice had not been received.

She emphasised that the Committee had never refused or delayed an in-camera hearing, nor had it taken any decision to that effect. Rather, the matter had remained pending due to the need for legal guidance.

Ms Sangoni further noted that Mr Dotyeni had subsequently made a protected disclosure in which he had publicly set out the information he had intended to present. She observed that this information was now in the public domain and had not, to her knowledge, been formally submitted to the Committee.

She suggested that the Committee consider whether the issues raised in the correspondence were distinct from those contained in the protected disclosure, or whether they had already been publicly disclosed. She reiterated that there was no basis for the assertion that the Committee had agreed to an in-camera hearing and subsequently failed to act. She proposed that the Committee revisit the original question regarding how such information should be handled within the applicable intelligence framework and, thereafter, determine an appropriate course of action.

There were no further contributions from ANC Members at that stage.

MKP

Mr Skosana addressed the matter concerning Ms Sarah-Jane Trent and stated that, in his view, the Committee should not revisit events retrospectively. He indicated that, from the perspective of the MKP, there was no basis to entertain the request. He emphasised that Ms Trent had not been compelled to testify and had confirmed her willingness and fitness to do so at the time. He cautioned that revisiting such matters could set an undesirable precedent, whereby other witnesses might subsequently seek to retract or challenge their testimony on similar grounds. He stated that this would undermine the integrity of the Committee’s work and render its processes ineffective.

Turning to the matter of Mr Dotyeni, Mr Skosana described it as a sensitive issue. He expressed concern that the Committee had not adequately addressed the matter, noting that discussions regarding a possible in-camera session had taken place but had not been concluded. He stated that the Committee had been placed in a difficult position, particularly where legal and procedural uncertainties arose, and suggested that the legal team should have provided clearer guidance in this regard.

Mr Skosana further remarked that the Committee risked being perceived as engaging in a cover-up, particularly where matters were not fully addressed. He emphasised that reliance on information circulating in the public domain, including social media, was inappropriate and that evidence should be formally presented before the Committee. He stressed that the Committee had been afforded a constitutional platform to ventilate issues and seek the truth, and that this responsibility should be exercised fully.

He called for self-reflection by Members, questioning whether the Committee had done justice to all witnesses who had appeared before it. He reiterated his earlier view that the Committee had only “scratched the surface” of the matters under consideration. He emphasised the importance of maintaining public confidence in the Committee and ensuring that its processes were not perceived as partisan or protective of particular individuals.

Mr Skosana expressed concern that certain matters, including those relating to the Inspector-General of Intelligence and Mr Dotyeni, appeared to remain unresolved as the Committee approached the finalisation of its report. He indicated that he did not recall receiving legal advice on these matters, despite earlier indications that such advice would be provided. He concluded by reiterating that the legal team should assist the Committee in addressing these outstanding issues.

There were no further contributions from MKP Members at that stage.

DA

Mr I Cameron (DA) indicated that he had no inputs at that stage.

EFF

Ms Mathys raised concerns regarding the return of the classified IGI report by the Speaker. She stated that it was troubling that, even if the Committee had not formally communicated with the Speaker, no attempt had been made by the Speaker to seek clarity before returning the report. She cautioned that this could give rise to various interpretations, including the perception that the matter was being diverted. She supported the proposal that the Chairperson engage with the Speaker to resolve the matter and facilitate access to the report.

On the issue of Ms Sarah-Jane Trent, Ms Mathys reiterated that the Committee had already taken a decision that the Rules of the National Assembly did not permit the expungement of testimony. She stated that Ms Trent was at liberty to pursue relief through the courts if she so wished. She further emphasised that all witnesses had been asked to confirm their willingness and fitness to testify, and that any medical concerns should have been raised prior to giving evidence. She concluded that there was no basis for the Committee to engage further on the matter.

With regard to the correspondence from the Busisiwe Mkhwebane Foundation, Ms Mathys stated that she did not recall any formal decision by the Committee to hold an in-camera hearing for Mr Dotyeni. She rejected any suggestion that the Committee had acted improperly in this regard.

She further indicated that Mr Dotyeni’s protected disclosure had, in fact, been submitted directly to the Committee, in addition to circulating in the public domain. She proposed that the Committee consider incorporating this disclosure into the evidentiary record. She suggested that, should any clarification be required, the Committee could request written responses from Mr Dotyeni and, if necessary, consider calling him at a later stage. She expressed the view that this approach would allow the Committee to proceed without delay while still addressing the substance of the matter.

FF+

Mr Wessels supported the proposal that the Chairperson engage with the Speaker regarding the IGI report.

On the matter of Ms Sarah-Jane Trent, he agreed that the Committee had already considered and resolved the issue, and that no new factors warranted reconsideration. He reiterated that any further remedy available to Ms Trent would lie within the courts.

With regard to the matter of Mr Dotyeni and the question of an in-camera hearing, Mr Wessels stated that he did not recall any formal decision by the Committee to convene such a session. He suggested that the Committee should consider the matter within the context of its broader deliberations on the report. He added that there appeared to be sufficient evidence before the Committee to allow it to reach conclusions and, if necessary, to recommend that the matter be referred for further scrutiny by another parliamentary structure.

Chairperson’s Resolution

The Chairperson stated that he would establish contact with the Office of the Speaker regarding the IGI report. He further indicated that, in relation to Ms Sarah-Jane Trent, the Committee would respond in accordance with the Rules of the National Assembly and its prior decision on the matter. He emphasised that the Committee would not accept or engage with confidential information that had been informally submitted, as this could expose the Committee to allegations of improper disclosure.

He referred to previous correspondence in which concerns had been raised regarding questions posed during proceedings, noting that the Committee should not create opportunities for further allegations of impropriety. He cautioned that the Committee should avoid providing any basis for external parties to impose conditions or pursue claims against it.

Turning to the matter concerning Mr Dotyeni, the Chairperson agreed with Members that no formal commitment had been made by the Committee to convene an in camera hearing. He stated that, while such a possibility had been discussed, no decision had been taken. He indicated that the Committee would respond to the Busisiwe Mkhwebane Foundation accordingly.

The Chairperson further expressed reservations about considering information that had not been formally presented before the Committee. He noted that reliance on information circulating in the public domain, including social media, would be inconsistent with the Committee’s established procedures. He emphasised that all information considered by the Committee should be formally submitted, typically by way of a statement or affidavit, and accompanied by a presentation in which the author assumes responsibility for the content.

He cautioned that reliance on unverified and unauthored material could undermine the integrity of the Committee’s work. He therefore urged Members to remain consistent in adhering to established practices and the applicable Rules.

The Chairperson then turned to the issue of the proposed extension and noted that Members appeared to support a timeframe extending to the end of May or the first or second week of June. He sought confirmation of this position.

ANC

Ms Sangoni indicated her agreement with the proposed timeframe.

MKP

Mr Skosana stated that, although he may have reservations, he would agree with the proposal, noting that there were limited alternatives available.

DA

Mr Cameron indicated his agreement.

EFF

Ms Mathys indicated her agreement.

FF+

Mr Wessels indicated his agreement.

Condolences to Mr Skosana

The Chairperson noted that Mr Wessels had indicated earlier that he would need to be excused before 19:00. He further informed Members that the Committee had recently received the sad news of the passing of Mr Skosana’s mother. He invited Mr Wessels, prior to his departure, to convey condolences.

FF+

Mr Wessels expressed his condolences to Mr Skosana and his family on behalf of the FF+. He stated that Mr Skosana and his family were in their thoughts and prayers and conveyed their deepest sympathy.

ANC

Ms Sangoni expressed her condolences, stating that it was deeply painful to once again extend sympathies to a colleague. She conveyed the ANC’s heartfelt condolences to Mr Skosana and his family on the passing of his mother. She further shared a message of comfort, expressing the hope that he would find strength and courage during this period of grief.

DA

Mr Cameron reiterated condolences on behalf of his party, noting that similar sentiments had been expressed previously. He acknowledged the difficulty of such moments and expressed the hope that Mr Skosana’s mother was now at peace.

The Chairperson enquired whether Members of the EFF were still present to convey condolences.

There was no response.

The Chairperson then confirmed that Mr Skosana was present and invited him to respond.

Mr Skosana expressed his gratitude to colleagues, staff, and all those who had extended messages of support during this difficult time. He described the passing of his mother as one of the most painful experiences of his life and acknowledged the comfort provided by the support he and his family had received.

He extended appreciation to those who had attended the funeral service and visited the family, noting in particular the presence of representatives from various organisations and political parties. He stated that their support had brought comfort and demonstrated a sense of unity and care. He extended special thanks to those who had attended the funeral service and visited the family. In particular, he conveyed his gratitude to Mr Malema and his delegation, the Deputy Secretary-General of the EFF; to Ms Mathys; and to the Premier of Gauteng, who had attended to offer their support. He concluded by thanking Members for their unwavering support and kindness.

The Chairperson conveyed condolences on behalf of the Committee and the Speaker of Parliament. He expressed sincere sympathy and extended prayers for strength and comfort to Mr Skosana and his family during their bereavement.

Adjournment

The Chairperson thanked Members and indicated that further communication would be issued regarding subsequent developments.

The meeting was adjourned.
