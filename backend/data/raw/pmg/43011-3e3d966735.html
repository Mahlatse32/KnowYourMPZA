Mkhwanazi Inquiry: Housekeeping Matters

Ad Hoc Committee to Investigate Allegations made by Lieutenant General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi

15 May 2026
Chairperson: Mr M Lekganyane (ANC)
Share this page:

Meeting Summary

Video

Ad Hoc Committee on Gen Mkhwanazi's Allegations

Acting Minister of Police Report (Confidential)

Draft Committee Report (Confidential)

The Ad Hoc Committee to Investigate Allegations made by Lieutenant General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi met to discuss progress on the evidence leaders’ report, the Committee’s own draft report, the timetable for finalising its work, and the handling of a classified intelligence report.

The Committee was advised that the evidence leaders had not met the earlier deadline for the evidentiary overview, but that substantial progress had been made and a draft was expected soon. The Committee expressed concern about the delay, but also recognised that the revised timetable would need to be managed carefully if the Committee was still to complete its work by 12 June. Members stressed that the schedule remained tight, and the Committee considered whether additional sitting time would be needed to study the evidence leaders’ report and prepare inputs before reconvening to finalise the broader report.

A major concern raised by the Committee was the status and usefulness of the report that had been returned by the Speaker and redirected to the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence. The Committee questioned whether it would be meaningful to view a report that could not be discussed openly, and whether access to it would materially assist the Committee in preparing its own findings. After further explanation of the protocol governing classified information, the Committee accepted that the report should be accessed through the proper Intelligence procedures and agreed that the relevant parliamentary structures should facilitate that process.

The Committee also received a briefing on the draft Committee report prepared by the content team. The Committee supported the circulation of the draft to allow Members time to study it, while emphasising the need for strict confidentiality and discipline to prevent any premature leakage.

Meeting report

The Chairperson commenced the meeting by conducting a roll call and greeting the Members present.

When greeting Ms Kohler Barnard, Adv G Breytenbach (DA) indicated that Ms Kohler Barnard was travelling.

The Chairperson then asked Mr Vhonani Ramaano, Committee Secretary, whether the number of Members present constituted a quorum.

Mr Ramaano confirmed that a quorum was present.

The Chairperson also greeted the other persons attending the meeting, including the evidence leaders, parliamentary staff, and members of the media.

Opening Remarks

The Chairperson stated that the meeting had been convened because, during the previous meeting, the Committee had considered a request from the evidence leaders for an extension until 8 May to submit the evidentiary overview. However, the evidence leaders had not been able to complete their work within the agreed timeframe. He explained that this had affected the calendar previously adopted by the Committee, as Members had been given until 15 May to submit their inputs on the two reports circulated to them. In the absence of a complete report from the evidence leaders, Members had not been in a position to submit their inputs, which in turn affected the subsequent deadlines set by the Committee.

The Chairperson further noted that the Speaker had extended the Committee’s deadline until 12 June. He recalled that, during the previous meeting, Members had agreed that 12 June should be the final day on which the Committee conducted its work. He added that he had also been informed that Parliament’s Programming Committee was planning for virtual sessions from June onwards, as political parties would likely be engaged in election campaigns.

He stressed that it was therefore necessary for Parliament’s work to be concluded before Members embarked on election campaigns, particularly as other developments could arise that might place additional pressure on Parliament’s programme. For that reason, he had considered it necessary to convene the meeting.

The Chairperson stated that, should the evidence leaders have any proposal or request to make to the Committee, it should be formally tabled before the Committee. This would enable the Committee to gain clarity on when the report could reasonably be expected and to determine how best to revise the programme leading up to 12 June. He emphasised, however, that any adjustments would have to be made on the understanding that 12 June remained the deadline the Committee was required to meet.

He further indicated that, after the evidence leaders’ presentation, he would invite Mr Tetyana to provide a brief report on the report previously submitted to Parliament by the Acting Minister. Before proceeding, he invited Members to indicate whether they wished to add any items to the agenda.

Members proposed no additional items.

The Chairperson noted that he did not observe any indications from Members wishing to add items to the agenda and therefore assumed that the Committee agreed to proceed with the agenda as circulated. He then requested apologies from Members unable to attend.

Apologies

Ms K Sangoni (ANC) stated that Mr M Ntuli (ANC) had been occupied with organisational work and was expected to join the meeting later. She added that she was still attempting to contact Ms T Sokanyile (ANC) and therefore did not yet have formal apologies to tender on their behalf.

The Chairperson enquired whether any apology had been received from the MKP.

Mr Ramaano confirmed that no apology had been received from the MKP.

The Chairperson then asked whether the Democratic Alliance had submitted any apologies.

Adv Breytenbach reiterated that Ms Kohler Barnard was travelling.

The Chairperson proceeded to ask whether any apologies had been received from the EFF.

Mr Ramaano confirmed that no apology had been received.

The Chairperson then enquired whether any apologies had been received from the IFP.

Mr Ramaano again confirmed that no apology had been received.

The Chairperson asked whether the PA had submitted any apologies.

Mr A Sauls (PA) responded that there were no apologies.

The Chairperson thanked Mr Sauls and welcomed him to the meeting, noting that, during the earlier exchange of pleasantries, it appeared that Mr Sauls may still have been experiencing connection difficulties.

Mr Sauls responded that he had, in fact, been present earlier, had responded, and had participated in the exchange of pleasantries.

The Chairperson replied that the Committee had not heard him earlier and remarked that he was still struggling to hear Mr Sauls clearly.

Mr Sauls then checked with the Chairperson whether he was more audible, and the Chairperson confirmed that his audio had improved slightly.

The Chairperson next requested apologies from the FF Plus.

Mr Ramaano informed the Committee that Mr W Wessels (FF+) was participating in a budget debate and might join the meeting should he conclude earlier.

The Chairperson then asked Ms D James (ActionSA) whether there were any apologies from ActionSA.

Ms D James (ActionSA) indicated that there were no apologies.

Evidence Leaders’ Presentation on Progress of the Report

The Chairperson invited Adv Arendse to raise any issues or requests before the Committee, particularly regarding any additional time required. He stated that the Committee would consider any proposal placed before it and, if necessary, revise the schedule accordingly. He reiterated, however, that the Committee was expected to complete its work by 12 June.

The Chairperson further stated that, once Adv Arendse had concluded his presentation, he could hand over to Mr Tetyana to provide the briefing referred to earlier when the agenda items had been introduced.

Adv Norman Arendse, Senior Counsel and Evidence Leader, commenced by apologising for not meeting the previous deadline. He explained that, during the week ending Friday, 8 May, he and one of his junior colleagues had been required to appear in person before a full bench of three judges in Pretoria. He noted that the matter had initially been scheduled to proceed virtually, but that, late on the preceding Thursday, they had been informed that physical attendance in court would be required. As a result, the week had effectively been unavailable for any other work, which had affected the finalisation of the draft report.

Adv Arendse further indicated that there were additional issues, which he had raised confidentially with the Chairperson, that had also contributed to the delay. Nevertheless, he stated that considerable progress had been made and that substantially more work had been completed than what had been reflected in the draft presented at the previous meeting, approximately two weeks earlier.

He advised the Committee that the evidence leaders were aiming to finalise the draft report by this coming Friday. In light of the extended deadline of 12 June, he proposed that, should the draft be submitted by Friday, 22 May, the Committee would then have the week commencing 25 May to consider and prepare its inputs, potentially by 29 May. Thereafter, the Committee could reconvene to discuss issues arising from Members’ consideration of the draft report and associated inputs.

Adv Arendse further proposed that the report could then be finalised during the week commencing 5 June, which, in his view, would allow the Committee to meet the 12 June deadline reasonably comfortably.

However, he requested clarity regarding the notice issued by the Speaker’s Office, specifically whether the Committee’s report had to be formally tabled in Parliament on or before 12 June, or whether the notice merely meant that the Committee’s mandate would terminate on that date. He indicated that clarity on this issue would be appreciated.

Presentation on Speaker’s Response

Mr Andile Tetyana, Parliamentary Legal Advisor, Constitutional and Legal Services Office, stated that there had not been any significant developments since the matter was previously discussed. He reminded Members that the issue had been included on the agenda during the Committee’s meeting of 28 April.

He explained that, on the same day, the Acting Minister of Police had written to the Committee, indicating that the report had originally been submitted to the Speaker on 21 April. However, on 24 April, the Minister’s office had received a telephone call from the Speaker’s Office requesting that the report be collected and instead submitted to the Chairperson of the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence, Ms S Izaks (ANC). Mr Tetyana stated that it appeared that the Minister had acted in accordance with those instructions.

Mr Tetyana further recalled that, during the previous meeting, the Chairperson had mandated the legal team to write to the Acting Minister explaining the rationale for the Committee’s view that the report should be delivered to the Speaker’s Office. Consequently, on 29 April, correspondence had been sent both to the Speaker and to the Acting Minister of Police, with the Speaker copied on the communication. In that correspondence, the Committee explained that the reason the report was required to be delivered to the Speaker’s Office was that the protocol governing the viewing of confidential and classified information vested custodianship of such information in the Speaker.

He added that the protocol also contained detailed provisions regarding the manner in which such material should be viewed, and noted that these provisions had since been incorporated into the Committee’s terms of reference, specifically paragraphs 2.13 to 2.19.

Mr Tetyana informed the Committee that no response had yet been received from the Acting Minister. He suggested that the apparent position of the Minister may have been that his letter of 28 April sufficiently explained the circumstances, namely that the Speaker’s Office had instructed that the report be redirected to the Chairperson of the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence.

Mr Tetyana further suggested that the Committee may wish to consider the fact that the report would only have been made available to Members on a “need-to-know” basis. This would mean that Members would merely be permitted to view the report and that the report itself would not form part of the Committee’s report.

He further explained that, based on the letters from the suspended Inspector-General of Intelligence, the report appeared to deal with matters relating to the procurement of properties by Crime Intelligence, the security vetting of Major-General Madondo, and the appointment of Brigadier Mokwele within SAPS Crime Intelligence.

Mr Tetyana stated that he raised these issues in light of the looming deadline of 12 June. He suggested that, during its deliberations, the Committee should consider whether access to the report was necessary for the completion of its work, particularly given that the contents of the report would not ultimately find expression in the Committee’s own report.

He also reminded Members that, during the suspended National Commissioner’s appearance before the Committee on 17 March, the Commissioner had informed the Committee that he, together with General Khumalo, had already appeared before the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence regarding the report, and that the explanations provided there had been satisfactory to that Committee. Mr Tetyana emphasised that he was merely placing the information before Members for their consideration during deliberations.

The Chairperson stated that, in essence, the Speaker had returned the report, as had been indicated during the previous meeting. He added that the Speaker had also provided written reasons for returning the report and had directed that it be submitted to the appropriate parliamentary committee, namely the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence.

He reminded Members that the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence had previously engaged with the Committee and that several meetings had been held with that Committee, including meetings involving the Chief Whip. He further explained that, should the report now be submitted to the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence, Members of the Committee would likely need to undergo a security evaluation in order to gain access to the report.

The Chairperson then addressed Mr Tetyana, indicating that he was uncertain whether Mr Tetyana had personally seen the report. However, if the matters identified by Mr Tetyana were indeed the contents of the report, Members would need to decide how much time consideration of the report would require and what impact those matters would have on the Committee’s deliberations on its own reports.

He further observed that, even if Members were permitted to view the report on a need-to-know basis, they would not be able to discuss its contents in Committee meetings, as doing so would violate the rules and protocols governing intelligence information. He stressed that the purpose of viewing the report would merely be for Members’ awareness and not for public discussion or deliberation in an open Committee meeting.

The Chairperson stated that no Member would be permitted to stand in the Committee and advance arguments based on the contents of the report they had viewed. He therefore questioned the materiality and practical usefulness of the report to the Committee’s work.

He added that he felt it necessary to supplement Mr Tetyana’s briefing, as Mr Tetyana, not being a politician, may have been constrained from expressing certain views directly.

The Chairperson summarised that two issues were now before the Committee. Firstly, the evidence leaders had requested an extension until the following Friday, by which date they undertook to submit the report. He noted that, should the Committee agree to the proposal, a revision of the programme would become necessary, including determining how much time Members would require to study the report and submit their inputs.

He further observed that it might not be possible to consider separately the report of the content team and that of the evidence leaders. Secondly, the Committee had before it the report presented by Mr Tetyana regarding the Acting Minister of Police’s report.

The Chairperson then invited comments and suggestions from Members on both matters.

Discussion

ANC

Ms Sangoni stated that the Committee should appreciate the reports that had been tabled before it. She described the delay as extremely disappointing and unfortunate. However, she acknowledged that Adv Arendse had indicated that there were matters on which he had been briefed and that, under the circumstances in which he had found himself, the delay appeared unavoidable. She therefore stated that the Committee effectively had little option but to accept the request for an extension.

Ms Sangoni recalled that, during the previous meeting, the Committee had agreed to allocate itself one week to consider the evidentiary report before reconvening to discuss it. She stated that she was uncertain about the volume of the report. Still, she noted that, if the report were to be received on 22 May, the Committee would have until 29 May to consider it, leaving approximately two weeks thereafter to finalise matters.

She described the proposed timeline as ambitious, but indicated that the ANC would support an approach aimed at ensuring that the Committee still met the relevant deadlines. She added that, if additional sitting days beyond the Committee’s ordinary schedule were required, this should be considered.

As Ms Sangoni began addressing the second issue relating to the Acting Minister of Police’s report, the Chairperson interjected and asked her first to address the related matter of revising the Committee’s schedule. He specifically asked how much time the Committee would require to study the evidence leaders’ report if the extension were to be granted.

Ms Sangoni stated that she believed she had already responded to the Chairperson’s question when she indicated that, within the extension period currently available, the Committee would need to make the process work, even if it required additional sitting days within the allocated timeframe. She noted that, based on her reading of the schedule, the Committee effectively had a two-week period within which to finalise its work.

She submitted that, if the Committee proceeded similarly to the hearings phase, it would likely need to sit three days per week, or as frequently as required. She expressed the hope that the Committee would still be able to conclude its work by 12 June, as previously determined. She emphasised that the ANC had consistently maintained its position that the Committee should not seek to extend the process beyond the current timeframe.

Ms Sangoni stated that, accordingly, if the report were received, the Committee would have one week to study it, followed by two weeks to deliberate as an Ad Hoc Committee and finalise its report. She acknowledged that the timeline was highly ambitious but maintained that it could and should be achieved.

On the second issue, Ms Sangoni indicated that she did not fully understand Mr Tetyana’s point and found it somewhat disconcerting. She stated that the Committee had been attempting to locate the applicable protocols governing the handling of intelligence-related matters and did not recall any provision stating that such information was for “need-to-know” purposes only in the sense described.

She added that the Committee had supported the position that the report should be made available to it, particularly in light of evidence from various witnesses, including the suspended Inspector-General of Intelligence, who had not appeared before the Portfolio Committee. She stated that certain witnesses had suggested that the contents of the report related to alleged cover-ups, which in turn had contributed to divisions within the SAPS.

Ms Sangoni stated that it was difficult for the Committee if it was expected to view information but not draw conclusions or use it in its deliberations. She argued that it did not make sense that Members would be permitted to access information but then be restricted from considering its implications. She indicated that, in the absence of a clear reference to such restrictions in the protocols, the ANC would wish to reconsider its position and return to the matter.

She stated that, as matters stood, if Members were unable to make use of the information, then the purpose of viewing the report was unclear. She therefore requested that Mr Tetyana be directed to the specific provisions in Parliament’s protocol on the handling of classified information, which stipulated that Members must disregard or not act upon information viewed on a need-to-know basis.

Ms Sangoni concluded that these were the ANC’s inputs on the two matters before the Committee.

DA

Adv Breytenbach stated that she did not wish to repeat herself, but noted that the proposed timelines were feasible. She stated that the Committee needed to proceed with its work and confirmed that the DA supported the proposal as set out. She indicated that she largely agreed with the submissions made by Ms Sangoni.

PA

Mr Sauls stated briefly that the PA was satisfied and supported the proposals made by Ms Sangoni on both issues.

ActionSA

Ms James stated that, while the delays were unfortunate, ActionSA was comfortable with the proposals that had been placed before the Committee in respect of both matters.

Further Engagement on Protocol and Speaker’s Response

The Chairperson asked Mr Tetyana whether he had heard the concerns raised by Ms Sangoni regarding the applicable protocol.

Mr Tetyana indicated that he had heard the concerns, but noted that Ms Mathys had also raised her hand to speak.

EFF

Ms L Mathys (EFF) apologised for her late arrival and explained that she had been delayed due to an organisational matter. She further conveyed apologies on behalf of Mr J Malema (EFF), who had been unable to attend the meeting as initially anticipated. She also apologised for not informing the Committee in advance.

Ms Mathys indicated that she might raise questions that had already been addressed and requested indulgence in that regard.

She stated that, in the previous meeting, the Committee had been informed that the Speaker had returned the intelligence report on the basis that she had not been informed that it was intended for Parliament. Ms Mathys expressed concern that the Speaker had not engaged the Chairperson or the Acting Minister of Police before returning the report, and suggested that this may have caused unnecessary delay.

She asked whether the Speaker had provided any further explanation for returning the report without first verifying its intended purpose with the Committee or the relevant parliamentary structures.

The Chairperson responded that the Committee had written to the Speaker in accordance with its earlier resolution. He explained that the Speaker had indicated in her correspondence that she had not been informed that the report was intended for Parliament. The Committee had therefore informed the Speaker that the Acting Minister had confirmed, in writing, that the report was intended for the Committee.

Ms Mathys indicated that her concern was specifically how the Speaker had responded to receiving the report from the Acting Minister, and why she had returned it without first engaging either the Chairperson or the Acting Minister. She stated that this had resulted in unnecessary delays. She noted that she would park the matter for the time being.

Ms Mathys then raised a further issue regarding additional resources for the evidence leaders. She asked whether there had been any update on a request to reinstate an additional evidence leader to assist in finalising the report, suggesting that delays may be linked to the non-availability of resources from Parliament.

The Chairperson responded that the matter of additional resources had been addressed in correspondence sent to the Speaker pursuant to a Committee resolution. He stated that the Speaker’s response indicated that the issue was administrative in nature and fell within the administration of Parliament and the Office of the State Attorney. He explained that the individual concerned had been seconded to Parliament by the State Attorney and that the period of secondment had expired.

He further stated that, in her response, the Speaker had indicated that the matter had been referred to the Secretary to Parliament for further handling. He asked Mr Tetyana to confirm or supplement the position and also to respond to the concerns raised regarding the protocol.

Parliamentary Legal Advisor’s Response

Mr Tetyana confirmed that there was no further information to add regarding the issue of additional resources. He stated that the Speaker’s letter of 12 May 2026 indicated that the correspondence had been conveyed to Parliament for consideration within the applicable financial framework.

On the issue of the intelligence report and protocol, Mr Tetyana stated that there was only one Parliament and one designated committee dealing with classified information, namely the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence. He explained that the protocol governed how such information was to be handled.

He referred to previous instances in which the protocol had been applied, including matters involving Colonel Kenan and the Standing Committee on Public Accounts (SCOPA) in relation to the arms deal. He explained that the protocol regulated access to classified information and stipulated, among other things, that only Members with a direct functional requirement could access such information. In contrast, alternate and co-opted Members were excluded.

He further stated that the protocol expressly prohibited the disclosure, copying, or reproduction of classified information, and that any unauthorised disclosure would constitute a breach of parliamentary privilege and contempt of Parliament.

Mr Tetyana clarified that the purpose of the “need-to-know” principle was not to prevent Members from acting on irregularities or wrongdoing, but rather to regulate the manner in which classified information was accessed and handled within Parliament. He emphasised that such matters would not be discussed in open session but within the appropriate procedural framework, including in closed meetings where necessary.

He further stated that the classification of information remained in place unless formally declassified by the Minister. He emphasised that the Committee was not being asked to ignore or disregard the report, but rather to comply with the established procedures governing classified information.

Mr Tetyana also noted that correspondence from the suspended Inspector-General of Intelligence had been shared with the Committee, and that such correspondence indicated the nature of the issues contained in the report. He confirmed, in response to the Chairperson, that he had not personally seen the full report, but was relying on the contents of the correspondence already provided to the Committee.

The Chairperson thanked Mr Tetyana for the clarification and invited Ms Mathys to continue with her contribution.

Ms Mathys stated that she noted the clarification provided during the discussion, adding that she had joined the meeting during that process. She indicated that the additional clarification was appreciated.

She proposed that the Committee should first convene to view the report in accordance with the applicable protocols. Thereafter, she suggested that the Ad Hoc Committee should meet in camera in order to deliberate on the contents of the report, as had been advised, and to determine how it should be incorporated into the broader Committee report to be presented to the National Assembly and to the public.

Ms Mathys further noted that the Committee’s legal counsel had confirmed that criminality could not be classified. She indicated that, in her view, such matters could be discussed in camera within the Ad Hoc Committee setting, where Members would be able to engage more substantively. She added that, should there be a basis for doing so, the Committee should be able to summon the Acting Minister to account for why the report remained classified, particularly if it contained indications of criminal conduct.

On the issue of timelines, Ms Mathys stated that it was difficult to determine the exact amount of time required, as Members had not yet had sight of the report. She indicated that the Committee should proceed on the basis of the proposed timelines for the time being, while remaining mindful of the time previously required to finalise the terms of reference, noting that a similar situation could arise.

She suggested, from an administrative perspective, that the technical team of Parliament should undertake a comparative analysis between the two reports, identifying similarities and differences. She stated that this would assist Members and facilitate more efficient deliberations during the Committee’s discussions.

Chairperson’s Response and Deliberations

The Chairperson noted Ms Mathys’ proposal that the report be made available in terms of the protocol of the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence. He indicated that he would first allow Members to respond, as Ms Mathys had been the last speaker on the matter.

ANC

Ms Sangoni stated that, from the outset, the Committee had sought engagement with the Inspector-General of Intelligence without success. She noted that access to the report had only been made possible following the intervention of the Minister. She added that the suspended Inspector-General had previously indicated that the report contained the substance of matters he would have raised with the Portfolio Committee.

Ms Sangoni submitted that it would amount to a serious miscarriage of justice if the Committee were not permitted to view the report. She further noted that various witnesses, including those making allegations against the PKTT and other matters under investigation, had relied on information contained in the classified report.

She concluded that the ANC fully supported that the Committee should be granted access to the report and that subsequent discussions should take place in accordance with the applicable protocol and the Committee’s terms of reference.

Democratic Alliance (DA)

Adv Breytenbach stated that she agreed that the Committee should be granted access to the report. She added that Members should be allowed to read it as far as possible and that this should occur as soon as possible, in order to ensure that the Committee was prepared for engagement on the broader report.

Patriotic Alliance (PA)

Mr A Sauls (PA) stated that the PA was in agreement with the proposal.

ActionSA

Ms D James (ActionSA) stated that ActionSA agreed with the proposal.

Further Deliberations

The Chairperson asked Ms Mathys whether she wished to respond, noting that the Committee had accepted the proposal that the protocol of the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence should be followed.

Ms Mathys responded that she had no further comments.

The Chairperson indicated that the proposal had originated from Ms Mathys and had therefore been returned to her for comment. He then directed Mr Tetyana to facilitate the application of the protocol with the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence, noting that the latter, as custodian of the protocol, would need to initiate the process to ensure compliance.

The Chairperson summarised that the Committee would write to the Joint Standing Committee on Intelligence to inform it of the matter and to request initiation of the process in accordance with the protocol.

He further stated that, upon receipt of the evidence leaders’ report, the Committee might need to convene a short meeting to formally “launch” or receive the report. He added that Members would then assess the volume of the report and determine the time required for consideration and input.

The Chairperson then asked Mr Vhonani Ramaano whether there were any announcements.

Input from Parliamentary Content Advisor

Ms Nicolette van Zyl Gous, Parliamentary Content Advisor, requested permission to address the Committee, indicating that she wished to clarify matters relating to the evidentiary overview and the Committee’s report.

The Chairperson granted permission.

Ms van Zyl Gous clarified that the evidentiary overview being prepared by Senior Counsel was intended to assist Members during deliberations. She distinguished this from the Committee’s own report, which was being drafted by the content team.

She informed Members that the draft Committee report was ready for distribution. She stated that it was a comprehensive document, which included aspects of the evidentiary overview, analysis of evidence, as well as draft findings and recommendations. She indicated that it would be beneficial for Members to begin reviewing the report.

She further stated that the report would likely be distributed later that weekend or, at the latest, by Monday.

The Chairperson welcomed the suggestion, noting that it demonstrated preparedness on the part of the content team and assisted the Committee in moving towards finalisation of its work.

Ms van Zyl Gous added that the report focused on governance and oversight issues within the Committee’s parliamentary mandate.

The Chairperson directed that the report be circulated to Members, emphasising that it should be treated as the final draft. He stated that the content team would be allowed to formally present the report at an appropriate time in a future meeting. He added that, for the present, circulation should proceed to allow Members to engage with the document.

He then invited Members to indicate whether they agreed with the proposal.

Members’ Responses

ANC

Ms Sangoni indicated that the ANC had no objection to the proposal.

DA

Adv Breytenbach confirmed agreement.

EFF

Ms Mathys agreed with the proposal, but requested that strict confidentiality be observed and that the report not be circulated outside the Committee, cautioning against premature media dissemination and emphasising the need for discipline among Members.

The Chairperson acknowledged that the point raised was valid.

PA

Mr Sauls indicated agreement.

The Chairperson’s further remarks were interrupted due to technical difficulties and were not clearly audible. Mr Sauls indicated that he would continue facilitating the meeting in the interim.

FF+

Mr W Wessels (FF+) indicated agreement with the proposal.

ActionSA

Ms James indicated that she was in agreement with the proposal and enquired about the Chairperson’s status, confirming her support for the motion.

Chairperson’s Response Following Technical Difficulties

The Chairperson indicated that he had resolved his technical issues and greeted Mr Wessels. He enquired whether Mr Wessels had been able to follow the proceedings of the meeting.

Mr Wessels responded that he had recently joined the meeting but had understood the general thrust of the discussion.

The Chairperson clarified that he had been present in the meeting but had initially been calling whilst on mute, and had only realised this later. He indicated that he would first attend to Ms James before proceeding to Mr Wessels, in order to ensure that all Members were aligned with the discussion.

Ms James reiterated that she was in agreement.

The Chairperson then invited Mr Wessels to indicate his position.

Mr Wessels confirmed that he was also in agreement.

Chairperson’s Further Remarks on Confidentiality and Circulation of Reports

The Chairperson noted that a concern had been raised by Members regarding the possible premature circulation or leakage of a draft report before it had been formally considered by the Committee.

He explained that the report referred to by Ms Nicolette van Zyl Gous was part of the Committee’s internal process and would follow the consideration of the evidentiary overview and related documents previously submitted by the content team. He emphasised that the draft report to be circulated from Monday would constitute a near-final version, which the Committee would ultimately consider and adopt for presentation to Parliament.

The Chairperson cautioned that, if such a report were to be leaked prior to formal deliberation, it would create serious difficulties for the Committee. He stressed that the Committee had so far managed its earlier reports responsibly and without incident, and urged Members to maintain the same discipline.

He further highlighted that the forthcoming draft report would contain substantive recommendations on the criminal justice system, as well as the Committee’s findings and observations, and therefore required careful handling.

The Chairperson questioned whether the Committee should proceed with circulation over the coming weekend or Monday, or whether it might be more appropriate to align the release of the draft Committee report with the receipt of the evidence leaders’ report. He cautioned that premature release could result in the Committee “putting the cart before the horse”.

He indicated that, while he had concerns about early circulation, he would defer to the will of the Committee should Members be strongly in favour of releasing the report earlier.

ANC

Ms Sangoni stated that the Committee understood the principle being raised, but that the Chairperson’s recommendation required further clarification. She indicated that the ANC supported the caution against any leakage of the report.

However, she emphasised that Members required adequate time to study and consider the draft report before engaging with it formally in Committee. She stated that it was her understanding that the earlier discussion had been that Ms van Zyl Gous would circulate the draft report, after which the Committee would reconvene at a later stage to deliberate and formally consider it.

Chairperson’s Clarification

The Chairperson reiterated that his concern was the possibility of the report being leaked before proper consideration by Members. However, he indicated that if Members were firmly of the view that the draft report should be circulated between the present date and Monday, he would defer to the collective decision of the Committee.

Ms Sangoni responded that the understanding was that the document constituted a draft report with no formal standing, and therefore it would be inappropriate for it to be leaked. She added that such conduct would be regrettable.

Decision of the Committee

The Chairperson then asked Members whether they agreed that the draft report should be circulated by Monday, and requested a response by acclamation.

Members present indicated their agreement.

Closure

The Chairperson confirmed that the draft report would be circulated to Members and declared that Members were released from the meeting. He thanked Members for their participation.

The meeting was adjourned.
