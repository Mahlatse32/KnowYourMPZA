Mkhwanazi Inquiry: Presentation of Evidentiary Report by Evidence Leaders

Ad Hoc Committee to Investigate Allegations made by Lieutenant General Nhlanhla Mkhwanazi

28 May 2026
Chairperson: Mr M Lekganyane (ANC)
Share this page:

Meeting Summary

Video

Ad Hoc Committee on Gen Mkhwanazi's Allegations

Draft Report and Executive Summary (not made available to the public)

The Ad Hoc Committee met to discuss the evidentiary report prepared by the evidence leaders and to determine how much time Members needed to study it before beginning deliberations. The meeting focused on the presentation of the report, the main findings arising from the evidence, and the Committee’s next procedural steps toward adopting its own report and submitting it to the Office of the Speaker.

The evidence leader explained that the report dealt with the disbandment of the Political Killings Task Team, the legal and constitutional questions surrounding the ministerial directive of 31 December 2024, and the broader governance crisis within the South African Police Service and related institutions. They also summarised evidence on the transfer of 121 case dockets, alleged political interference, procurement irregularities in the health risk management services tender, corruption concerns, and the role of criminal networks in influencing public institutions. The presentation emphasised that the record disclosed systemic failures, questionable decision-making, and serious concerns about oversight, accountability, and procurement integrity.

Members then turned to their concerns and inputs as a Committee. The Committee welcomed the work done by the evidence leaders, but indicated that the report was substantial and required careful study before any detailed deliberations could begin. The Committee also signalled that it would need a structured method for engaging the report, with Members considering whether the discussion should proceed witness by witness, chapter by chapter, or by another organised approach. There was broad agreement that the Committee should not rush the process and should allow sufficient time for reflection, internal consultation, and preparation ahead of the deliberative stage.

The Committee further stressed that its main task would be to test the report carefully, interrogate the evidence, and then develop recommendations grounded in the record before it. Members agreed to take a week to study the report and to reconvene for a short preparatory meeting to decide on the methodology for deliberations.

Meeting report

Opening Remarks

The Chairperson greeted the Members present, the evidence leaders, parliamentary staff, and all other persons attending the meeting. He noted that Mr Nqola and Mr Ntuli were present and indicated that the Committee therefore had a quorum.

Mr C Shongwe (MKP) confirmed his presence in the meeting.

The Chairperson remarked that he had already greeted Members when calling their names during the attendance register.

Mr Shongwe apologised and explained that he had only just logged into the meeting.

Apologies

Ms K Sangoni (ANC) informed the Committee that Mr Nqola was present at the meeting but had conveyed his apologies as he would be attending the farewell function of Justice Tshiqi and might therefore be in and out of the meeting.

Mr Shongwe advised that Mr Skosana would be joining the meeting shortly. He further indicated that Mr Nomvalo was still occupied with party-related work.

Mr Vhonani Ramaano, Committee Secretary, informed the Committee that apologies had been received from Mr Malema and Ms Mathys, who had indicated that they had prior commitments.

Agenda

The Chairperson requested that the agenda be displayed on the screen, which was subsequently done.

The Chairperson explained that the meeting had been convened following the receipt of the evidence leaders' report. He noted that the report had been submitted on either Sunday or Monday, with the latest updated version having been circulated earlier that day. He stated that the purpose of the meeting was to provide the evidence leaders with an opportunity to present their report and to allow Members to determine how much time they required to study it.

He further explained that, once Members had indicated the time needed to consider the report, the Committee would schedule dates for deliberations. These deliberations would continue until the Committee reached the stage of adopting the report and submitting it to the Office of the Speaker. He added that, following discussion and adoption of the report, the Committee would also need to determine the way forward, including sharing the report with parties that had appeared before the Committee so that they could take note of the Committee's recommendations.

The Chairperson emphasised that the immediate priority was to receive the presentation and decide on the period required for Members to study the report. He noted that Members might wish to undertake consultations, discuss the report within their respective caucuses or study groups, and then return to the Committee for deliberations.

He stated that the item before the Committee remained an outstanding matter from the Committee's previous meeting. He invited Members to indicate whether they wished to add any further items to the agenda.

There were no additional items proposed for inclusion on the agenda.

Draft Report

The Chairperson indicated that he would hand over proceedings to the evidence leaders. He thanked Adv Arendse and his team, including Adv Mkhize and Adv Mokhaoetsi, for the substantial work undertaken in preparing the report. He acknowledged that the process had not been easy and expressed the Committee's appreciation for their efforts in reaching the current stage, where Members were able to consider the evidence leaders' report.

Before proceeding to the presentation, the Chairperson enquired whether Mr Tetyana wished to make any remarks.

Mr Andile Tetyana, Parliamentary Legal Advisor, Constitutional and Legal Services Office, indicated that he had no remarks to make.

Adv Norman Arendse, Evidence Leader and Senior Counsel, informed the Committee that the document before Members was a draft report, which would only become a final report once the Committee had satisfied itself that its contents reflected its views and that any conclusions, findings, and recommendations contained therein accorded with the wishes of the Ad Hoc Committee. He anticipated that Members would engage in further discussions and deliberations on the report in the coming days.

Adv Arendse apologised for the late submission of the report. He explained that the document was substantial and had required extensive interrogation, reading, verification, and cross-checking. He added that this process would continue as the Committee considered the report. Referring to an earlier email, he advised that the evidence leaders might, from time to time, submit further amendments and would indicate where such amendments had been made.

He noted that Members had received two versions of the same report. The first version, which had been circulated late on Sunday, had been structured around four themes covered in the report, with the evidence grouped under those themes by witness. Following further discussion, the evidence leaders had been persuaded that the revised version circulated that day would be more reader-friendly and more conducive to the Committee's deliberations.

Adv Arendse stated that the table of contents had been updated to improve ease of reference. He explained that Members should be able to navigate directly to specific sections, such as the evidence of Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi on page 39, by using hyperlinks. Should any technical issues arise, the evidence leaders would ensure that the functionality was made available?

He further advised that Section D had been introduced and was likely the most important section for the Committee's purposes. This section contained a summary of the evidence of each witness, presented on a witness-by-witness basis, with each witness's evidence organised according to the relevant topics addressed. He contrasted this with the earlier draft, which had grouped evidence according to broad themes and then placed witnesses under those themes. He expressed the hope that the revised format would provide a more convenient and coherent reading experience.

Adv Arendse reported that the summaries of the evidence of Lieutenant General Senthumule and Mr Robert McBride had been updated. He further noted that the report now included a summary of the President's responses to the significant questions posed by Members, which had been incorporated into the document.

He added that the recommendations had been updated to reflect references to the relevant Ministers of Police, including Deputy Minister Boshielo, Acting Minister Cachalia, and former Minister Cele. He further noted that a conclusion had now been included in the report. However, he emphasised that these were not the evidence leaders' own conclusions but rather conclusions arising from the evidence heard by the Committee.

Adv Arendse informed Members that the evidence leaders had prepared an executive summary. Although he had initially considered making it available prior to the meeting, he felt that doing so might have been excessive. He therefore proposed that the executive summary be circulated immediately after the meeting. Alternatively, he suggested that Adv Mkhize provide it to Mr Ramaano and Mr Tetyana during the course of the meeting for distribution to Members.

Turning to the contents of the executive summary, Adv Arendse explained that it commenced with the background to the establishment of the inquiry. He recalled that Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi's public briefing on 6 July 2025 had ultimately led to the establishment of the Ad Hoc Committee by Parliament, in terms of Rule 169(1), through a resolution adopted on 23 July 2025. The Committee had subsequently commenced hearing evidence on 7 October 2025, beginning with the testimony of Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi.

Adv Arendse stated that a central focus of the inquiry had been the Political Killings Task Team (PKTT), which had been established in July 2018. He explained that the President had established an Inter-Ministerial Committee (IMC) comprising Ministers responsible for Police, State Security, Defence, Justice, and Correctional Services. In turn, the PKTT had been established as a national, integrated, multidisciplinary team.

He noted that the President had clarified in his affidavit that he had not directly established the PKTT. Rather, the IMC had done so through the powers vested in its constituent ministries. The President had further clarified that he did not possess the legal authority to establish a South African Police Service (SAPS) task team directly.

Adv Arendse explained that the PKTT operated within a multi-layered governance structure. It reported to a Provincial Steering Committee, which in turn reported to a National Steering Committee, then to the IMC, and ultimately to the President. He further noted that the PKTT had continued to exist during the Seventh Administration because the IMCs were constituted according to ministerial portfolios rather than individual political office-bearers. He added that the President had clarified this position in his responses to Members' questions.

Adv Arendse highlighted that evidence heard on 5 September 2023 had revealed that the IMC had resolved that the PKTT should not be dissolved and should continue its operations. He recalled that the President's responses had included minutes of the IMC, which the Committee had seen for the first time, and that this resolution was reflected in those minutes.

With regard to the operational performance of the PKTT, Adv Arendse noted that the record was substantial. Since its inception, the Task Team had investigated 612 dockets, arrested and charged 436 suspects, recovered 156 firearms, and ballistically linked 55 of those firearms to politically related cases. In addition, 128 accused persons had been found guilty in 106 cases, while 29 accused persons had received life sentences. Overall, sentences imposed amounted to more than 1 800 years of imprisonment.

Adv Arendse further noted that the National Commissioner had approved a 12-month budget extension in April 2024 and had endorsed in writing that the Task Team should continue operating beyond the election period for as long as there remained work to be done. He indicated that this information had emerged from the evidence of Acting National Commissioner Dimpane, who also held the substantive position of Chief Financial Officer.

Adv Arendse stated that a significant area of debate throughout the inquiry had concerned the ministerial directive issued on 31 December 2024 by Minister Mchunu while National Commissioner Masemola was on leave. He explained that the directive consisted of three components: the immediate disbandment of the PKTT, a moratorium on the filling of vacant posts within Crime Intelligence, and the development of a new SAPS approach to ports of entry.

He noted that the directive had been issued at 17:20 on New Year's Eve and that the National Commissioner had only become aware of it through social media and WhatsApp messages. Adv Arendse indicated that Members would need to consider the constitutional and legal framework governing the relationship between the Minister and the National Commissioner in determining whether the Minister had been authorised to issue the directive.

In this regard, he referred Members to section 206(1) of the Constitution, which empowers the Minister to determine national policing policy after consultation with provincial governments; section 207, which requires the National Commissioner to exercise control and management of SAPS in accordance with national policy and the directions of the Minister; section 205(3), which sets out SAPS's operational mandate to prevent, combat and investigate crime, maintain public order and protect inhabitants; and section 71 of the SAPS Act, which vests responsibility for the organisation of the Service in the National Commissioner rather than the Minister.

Adv Arendse stated that General Masemola's central submission had been that there was a clear constitutional distinction between the Minister's political responsibilities and the National Commissioner's operational authority. According to General Masemola, the disbandment of an active multidisciplinary task team crossed that constitutional boundary.

He further observed that several witnesses had confirmed the absence of prior consultation before the directive was issued. The National Commissioner had not been consulted and had learned of the directive through social media. Similarly, the President had confirmed that he had neither been consulted nor approved the disbandment and had also learned of it through social media. In addition, neither the National Prosecuting Authority (NPA) nor the State Security Agency (SSA), both of which were partners in the PKTT, had been consulted. The Director-General of the Civilian Secretariat had likewise not been engaged, despite a legal requirement for consultation.

Adv Arendse noted that Minister Mchunu had informed the Portfolio Committee on Police on 5 March 2025 that the President had been consulted and had been, in his words, "in agreement" with the decision. However, Adv Arendse stated that the evidence before the Committee had shown that assertion to be unfounded. The Minister had later characterised the interaction with the President as a brief meeting that took place after the directive had already been issued, during which no reasons or supporting documentation had been provided.

Adv Arendse indicated that the Committee would need to assess the credibility of Minister Mchunu's stated reasons for issuing the directive. He explained that the Minister had advanced four broad categories of justification: complaints raised by Professor and Ms Mashale; the strategic strengthening of permanent SAPS structures; the allegedly time-bound nature of task teams; and budgetary constraints. According to Adv Arendse, the evidence presented to the Committee had materially undermined each of these justifications.

Adv Arendse stated that Minister Mchunu had conceded that he had never met Ms Mashale and that the Inspector-General had advised him that between 75% and 80% of her allegations were untrue. He further noted that the National Commissioner had informed the Minister that Ms De Haas's allegations were "not as she raises them". Despite this, both sets of social media posts had been annexed to Minister Mchunu's statement.

Adv Arendse explained that the 2019 work study relied upon by the Minister did not mention the PKTT. Furthermore, the Marais Report had recommended an impact analysis rather than the disbandment of the Task Team. He noted that, as the evidence had shown, the Minister had applied that recommendation only to the PKTT, despite the report addressing 13 other task teams.

He further stated that the SAPS Chief Financial Officer, now Acting National Commissioner Lieutenant General Dimpane, had confirmed that she had held no discussions with the Minister regarding the PKTT budget and had learned of the directive through media reports. Adv Arendse added that the evidence had directly contradicted the Minister's assertion that the PKTT had operated unlawfully after 2022. He referred Members to annexure "SM31" to the Minister's statement, which recorded the National Commissioner's express extension of the task team.

Adv Arendse noted that considerable time had been spent during the hearings debating the meaning of the word "immediately", although the issue itself was a serious one. He explained that the Minister's directive had required the PKTT to be disbanded "immediately". The Minister had repeatedly sought to resist the ordinary meaning of the term during his evidence; however, the Chairperson had ultimately ruled that its established meaning before the Committee was "now, now". Adv Arendse added that Lieutenant General Sibiya had confirmed that, in his understanding, "immediately" meant "now" and had adopted the same interpretation.

Adv Arendse stated that another contentious issue before the Committee had been the transfer of 121 dockets from KwaZulu-Natal to SAPS Head Office in Pretoria. He explained that 118 dockets had initially been transferred on 28 March 2025, with a further three transferred on 23 April 2025, bringing the total to 121. A subsequent audit had found that no substantive progress had been made on any of the cases during the five months that the dockets were held at Head Office. He noted that arrests that had already been authorised were not executed and that the dockets had ultimately been returned on the direction of the National Commissioner in August 2025.

Adv Arendse stated that the report also examined the alleged corrupt relationship between Minister Mchunu, Mr Brown Mogotsi, and Mr Vusimuzi "Cat" Matlala.

With regard to Mr Mogotsi, Adv Arendse explained that he was an ANC political associate with no formal role within SAPS. Nevertheless, he had been found to be in possession of sensitive SAPS intelligence and had become aware of the contents of the 31 December 2024 directive within hours of its transmission. Adv Arendse referred to WhatsApp messages dated 1 January 2025, which showed Mr Mogotsi informing Mr Matlala that he had arranged a meeting between Lieutenant General Sibiya and the Minister and stating that they "must have a solution" because "the task team that came to your house has been dissolved".

Adv Arendse noted that Minister Mchunu had informed the Portfolio Committee on Police on 5 March 2025 that he did not know Mr Mogotsi. However, when appearing before the Ad Hoc Committee on 16 October 2025, the Minister had twice confirmed that he knew Mr Mogotsi, stating, "Yes, I know him."

He further stated that the evidence had established that Mr Mogotsi had run the Minister's ANC branding campaign in 2017, had maintained regular contact with the Minister via WhatsApp and Signal, and had introduced himself to various parties as someone operating from within the Minister's office. Adv Arendse added that the Committee had, on several occasions, indicated to Mr Mogotsi that it did not consider him to have been truthful regarding the nature of his relationship with the Minister.

Turning to Mr Matlala, Adv Arendse noted that he had been arrested on 14 May 2025 on three counts of attempted murder and was a central figure in the alleged criminal syndicate referred to by Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi in his July 2025 media briefing.

Adv Arendse stated that the evidence had established several significant facts regarding Mr Matlala. He noted that Mr Matlala had an extensive criminal history spanning approximately 25 years. While he had initially presented his criminal record to the Committee as consisting only of a conviction for possession of stolen property, he had later conceded under questioning by Members that the conviction had in fact been for burglary, housebreaking and theft, for which he had served a term of direct imprisonment.

Adv Arendse further stated that Mr Matlala had admitted making cash payments totalling R500 000 to former Minister Bheki Cele. These payments comprised R300 000 in January 2025 and R200 000 in March 2025, allegedly as a facilitation fee to secure the return of his confiscated firearms and the cessation of police operations against him. Under questioning by Members, Mr Matlala had conceded that the payments were unlawful and amounted to bribery.

Adv Arendse observed that the allegations of bribery against former Minister Cele would likely require further investigation, particularly as Mr Cele had not subsequently been afforded an opportunity to respond to the allegations before the Committee.

Adv Arendse further noted that Mr Matlala had conceded that appointing his sister as a director of Medicare 24 Tshwane District while retaining effective control of the company amounted to fronting. Mr Matlala had acknowledged that another individual was effectively controlling the company behind the scenes and had accepted that this arrangement was improper.

He added that Mr Matlala had maintained a close personal relationship with a known manufacturer of illegal narcotics, Mr Jerry Boshoga, for at least two years without reporting the unlawful activities to any authority. Mr Matlala had also admitted lending approximately R1 million to Mr Boshoga.

Adv Arendse further noted that Mr Matlala travelled in a heavily protected convoy consisting of six vehicles and up to 14 personal protectors drawn from former Special Task Force and SANDF Special Forces personnel, at an estimated monthly cost of approximately R2 million. He stated that these facts had emerged during Mr Matlala's cross-examination before the Committee.

Turning to the R360 million Health Risk Management Services tender, Adv Arendse explained that Medicare 24 Tshwane District (Pty) Ltd, owned by Mr Matlala, had been awarded a three-year SAPS Health Risk Management Services contract in June 2024 valued at approximately R360 million.

He stated that the company had no operational track record in providing medical services between 2018 and 2024. Its claimed qualifications for the tender had been based solely on the track record of a separate entity, Medicare24 Holdings.

Adv Arendse reported that both the preliminary audit report of January 2025 and the final Audit and Risk Report of April 2025 had identified critical failures in the procurement process. These included evidence of fronting and undisclosed third-party arrangements. The franchise agreement had been unsigned, and the bidder had submitted the company profile of the franchisor, which had not itself bid for the contract, instead of the required third-party supplier declaration.

He further noted that forensic analysis had established that the signature appearing on the pricing schedule submitted with the bid differed from the signature on the original documents. Lieutenant General Fani, who had chaired the Bid Adjudication Committee (BAC), had conceded during his evidence that this discrepancy should have resulted in the bidder's disqualification.

Adv Arendse stated that the Bid Evaluation Committee (BEC) lacked the necessary evaluation expertise and that individual bid-scoring sheets could not be located. He added that a failure of due diligence had also characterised the procurement process.

He explained that allegations linking Mr Matlala to the Tembisa Hospital scandal and to matters associated with the murder of the late Babita Deokaran had already been in the public domain before the evaluation process took place. However, these issues had not been incorporated into the BEC's due diligence methodology.

Adv Arendse further noted that the BAC had undertaken no independent verification, had requested no additional documentation, and had produced no written reasons for its decision. Instead, it had simply recorded a one-line approval stating, "Approved". In this regard, Lieutenant General Fani had conceded that the tender should not have been awarded.

Adv Arendse stated that an IDAC warning had been transmitted to the National Commissioner in May 2024, indicating that the tender was linked to irregularities associated with the Tembisa Hospital matter and that the bidder lacked a valid tax clearance certificate. However, this information had never been communicated to Lieutenant General Fani, and the contract had nevertheless been signed.

Regarding irregular expenditure, Adv Arendse noted that Lieutenant General Dimpane had disclosed approximately R48 million in irregular expenditure in the 2024/25 annual financial statements, representing payments made under the contract from its inception until its cancellation in 2025. He added that all further payments had been halted and that a forensic investigation remained ongoing.

Adv Arendse stated that the evidence leaders had recommended that Lieutenant General Fani be referred to IDAC for further investigation and possible prosecution for breaches of the Public Finance Management Act (PFMA). He explained that Lieutenant General Fani had signed off on the award of the tender under delegation from the National Commissioner despite significant procurement irregularities identified in both the BEC and the BAC, which he himself had chaired. Adv Arendse noted that there was currently an ongoing criminal case involving suspended National Commissioner Masemola.

Turning to the allegations of organised crime syndicate infiltration, Adv Arendse stated that Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi had alleged the existence of the so-called "Big Five" criminal syndicate. According to Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi, the syndicate comprised tenderpreneurs with significant government contracts who worked in concert with law enforcement officials to protect cartel operations. He had publicly identified Mr Matlala and Mr Katiso Molefe as two members of the alleged syndicate, while declining to name the remaining three pending their appearance before the courts.

Adv Arendse noted that, when Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi returned to testify before the Committee on 18 March 2026, he identified additional persons of interest, namely Mr Steve Motsumi, Mr Suleiman Karim, Mr Stewart Shanik, and Mr Morgan Maumela.

Adv Arendse then addressed the Gauteng Counter-Intelligence Operations. He explained that, in April 2024, the PKTT had seconded 10 members to assist Gauteng organised crime investigations following the assassination of Mr Armand Swart, a Transnet service provider. These investigations had allegedly exposed a syndicate involving SAPS detectives, metropolitan police officers, and cartel members, including Mr Molefe and Mr Matlala.

He noted that Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi's position was that the disbandment of the PKTT had been triggered by its work in Gauteng rather than by its KwaZulu-Natal mandate.

Turning to allegations concerning the infiltration of the criminal justice system, Adv Arendse stated that evidence before the Committee included allegations that criminal syndicates had infiltrated various institutions, including SAPS, the metropolitan police, the NPA, IDAC, the magistracy, and Correctional Services.

He noted that Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi had alleged that SAPS infiltration extended to a Deputy National Commissioner and senior Crime Intelligence officials. Particular concerns had also been raised regarding the Ekurhuleni Metropolitan Police Department (EMPD).

Adv Arendse further stated that Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi had characterised a section of the NPA and IDAC as a "rogue section". In relation to the magistracy, evidence had been presented regarding unusual bail conditions granted in the Khumalo matter, which allegedly permitted cartel-linked individuals access to Crime Intelligence offices.

He added that Lieutenant General Khumalo had testified that criminal cartels actively cultivated relationships with senior officials in order to receive advance warning of law enforcement actions and to influence the deployment of state resources.

Turning to IDAC, its mandate, conduct, and the arrest of Lieutenant General Khumalo, Adv Arendse explained that IDAC had originally been established through a Presidential Proclamation in 2019 and had subsequently been made permanent through amendments to the National Prosecuting Authority Act. Its mandate covered serious, high-profile, complex corruption, commercial, and financial crimes. He emphasised that IDAC could only investigate matters referred to it and did not possess the power to initiate investigations on its own accord.

Adv Arendse stated that Mr Adams had lodged complaints in October 2024 concerning alleged criminal activities within Crime Intelligence. These complaints had subsequently been formally referred to IDAC by the Minister's Chief of Staff, Mr Nkabinde.

He further noted that, on 26 June 2025, IDAC had arrested Lieutenant General Dumisani Khumalo and six co-accused on charges of corruption and fraud relating to the irregular appointment of a Crime Intelligence official. The allegations had been framed as an instance of state capture involving the diversion of state resources for private gain.

Adv Arendse stated that several witnesses had raised concerns regarding IDAC's conduct and had questioned whether it had exceeded its mandate. Lieutenant General Khumalo, for example, had alleged that his arrest in relation to a permanent staff appointment matter fell outside IDAC's core mandate and had been intended to distract and intimidate the PKTT.

Adv Arendse further noted that, when Mr Matlala's cellular telephones had been handed to IDAC for forensic downloading, they had arrived already powered on, contrary to accepted forensic protocols. An IDAC cyber expert had reportedly observed that a substantial amount of data had been deleted. No final cyber-forensic report had been available at the time of testimony.

He added that, prior to October 2025, IDAC had no external oversight body beyond internal NPA oversight, ethical supervision, and parliamentary reporting. Advocate Johnson had confirmed this gap. Adv Arendse noted, however, that a judge had subsequently been appointed to fulfil an oversight role.

Turning to Crime Intelligence, Adv Arendse stated that evidence had revealed significant instability and governance failures. Lieutenant General Khumalo had testified that, upon assuming office as Divisional Commissioner of Crime Intelligence in January 2023, he had encountered widespread abuse of state vehicles, poor controls over informer rewards, the existence of ghost employees, and weak consequence management at senior levels.

Adv Arendse further noted that the moratorium imposed on the filling of Crime Intelligence vacancies by the directive of 31 December 2024 had left four provinces without provincial heads and had resulted in numerous critical operational vacancies. According to the evidence, the moratorium had been unexpected and had not been discussed during prior ministerial meetings.

He stated that Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi had identified the Crime Intelligence Secret Service Account, described by both Mr McBride and Mr O'Sullivan as chronically misused, as a structural enabler of corruption. According to Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi, control over the fund had been used to influence police officers, generals, and politicians.

Adv Arendse then addressed allegations of political interference. He stated that Lieutenant General Vuma had provided extensive evidence regarding ministerial interference in SAPS operations under previous Ministers.

According to her evidence, former Minister Cele had exerted pressure on SAPS to award PPE contracts during the COVID-19 pandemic to suppliers identified by him. Lieutenant General Vuma had resisted these efforts.

She had also testified regarding attempts to influence the RT15 cellular telephone contract and the Independent Development Trust (IDT) infrastructure arrangement in favour of particular service providers, contrary to PFMA requirements.

Adv Arendse further noted allegations concerning the procurement of IVU3 bulletproof vests. Evidence suggested that the vests had been purchased at approximately R33 000 each despite market prices ranging between R3 000 and R8 000. There were also allegations that luxury gifts had been distributed to officials who attended related events, including Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi and General Masemola. He noted that these allegations were reportedly the subject of an ongoing IDAC investigation.

Adv Arendse stated that Lieutenant General Vuma had characterised SAPS as an institution whose leadership had effectively collapsed, with successive National Commissioners having been subjected to instability resulting from cadre deployment and politicisation.

Turning to the evidence leaders' credibility assessments, Adv Arendse stated that they had concluded that Minister Mchunu's evidence raised significant concerns. He noted that the Minister had issued the directive using the word "immediately" but had subsequently struggled to reconcile his interpretation of that term with its ordinary meaning. The Minister had also advanced reasons during his testimony that had not been included in the directive itself and had relied on allegations from Dr De Haas and Ms Mashale, despite having been informed by both the Inspector-General and the National Commissioner that those allegations were unreliable.

Adv Arendse further noted that Minister Mchunu had admitted instructing his Chief of Staff to record Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi without consent and had misrepresented to Parliament the extent of his consultation with the President regarding the directive.

With regard to General Masemola, Adv Arendse stated that he had generally presented as a credible and consistent witness concerning the constitutional framework and the operational implications of the directive. However, two notable concessions had emerged. Firstly, General Masemola had accepted that the question of whether the Minister had the lawful authority to issue the directive was ultimately a matter for the Committee. Secondly, there appeared to be a meaningful difference in emphasis between his testimony before the Committee and the affidavit he had filed in the litigation instituted by Lieutenant General Sibiya following his suspension. Adv Arendse suggested that this discrepancy warranted closer examination.

Adv Arendse stated that the evidence of Mr Matlala had not been credible in several material respects. The evidence led the leaders to regard him as a participant in, rather than a victim of, the alleged corrupt network. His admissions under oath, including the R500 000 in cash payments to a former Minister of Police, the fronting arrangement within his company, the employment of a BEC evaluator after the tender award, and his association with a narcotics manufacturer, all warranted continued investigation by the NPA, IDAC, and the SIU.

Regarding Lieutenant General Fani, Adv Arendse stated that the evidence leaders had concluded that he had abdicated his responsibilities as Chairperson of the BAC. He had received the BEC report, approved it through a one-line endorsement, and signed the appointment letter without conducting any independent adjudication. His formal denial that the BAC had failed to exercise due care was irreconcilable with several concessions he had made during testimony, including his admission that "the tender should not have been awarded".

Adv Arendse stated that the evidence established that the President had not approved the disbandment of the PKTT and had not been consulted beforehand. He noted that the President's subsequent actions, including establishing the Madlanga Commission, placing Minister Mchunu on special leave and appointing an Acting Minister under section 98 of the Constitution, were constitutionally grounded.

However, Adv Arendse observed that the evidence leaders had identified systemic shortcomings in executive oversight. These included the absence of performance assessments for the Minister of Police during the Seventh Administration, a 14-month delay in acting on the fitness inquiry requested by former National Director of Public Prosecutions Adv Batohi concerning Director of Public Prosecutions Adv Chauke, and a failure to enforce a November 2023 Cabinet resolution requiring written reporting by the PKTT.

Adv Arendse stated that several forward-looking recommendations had emerged from the evidence. Vetting and integrity measures featured prominently throughout the proceedings, including independent lifestyle audits and mandatory pre-employment vetting for all senior SAPS appointments in accordance with section 17E of the SAPS Act.

He further recommended the continuation and strengthening of the PKTT, the establishment of a similar multidisciplinary capacity in Gauteng, and the institutionalisation of a prosecution-guided multidisciplinary investigative model.

With regard to procurement, Adv Arendse stated that the evidence pointed to the need for substantial reform. In particular, the findings concerning Lieutenant General Fani, the Medicare 24 tender, and irregularities within forensic procurement processes demonstrated the need for a comprehensive overhaul.

Regarding Crime Intelligence, he recommended legislative clarification and codification of its mandate, together with reforms to the accountability framework governing the Secret Service Account.

Adv Arendse noted that an issue repeatedly raised by Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi, namely the role of General Khan, had not been fully explored because General Khan had not been called to testify before the Committee.

In relation to IPID, Adv Arendse stated that it was important that the institution remain structurally independent from the Minister. He referred to the Glenister principles, which similarly applied to the Hawks.

Regarding the NPA and IDAC, he stated that consideration should be given to overhauling the legislative framework, expanding IDAC's mandate to address systemic issues, and ensuring that the role of the inspecting judge was fully operationalised. He further noted that several important recommendations had been advanced by Adv Batohi and warranted serious consideration.

Adv Arendse also emphasised the importance of introducing a statutory requirement obliging incoming National Commissioners to present structural reform plans to the relevant Portfolio Committee within prescribed timeframes.

In relation to technology, he highlighted the need for digital docket management systems, body-worn cameras, and data-driven policing methods to reduce opportunities for interference and corruption.

Adv Arendse then identified several areas that had not been fully explored during the inquiry. These included allegations of criminal infiltration within Correctional Services and the judiciary. He recalled that one acting judge had been mentioned during the proceedings, but no other judges or magistrates had been identified.

Other outstanding issues included the evaluation of chain-of-custody procedures, data integrity and digital evidence management processes.

Adv Arendse further noted that the Inspector-General of Intelligence's classified report concerning Crime Intelligence property irregularities had been proposed for consideration in a closed session, but this had not ultimately taken place.

He also referred to the Jermaine Prim letter, which had been introduced as new evidence by Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi during his appearance on 18 March 2026. As the letter had only been produced on the morning of the hearing, the evidence leaders had been unable to verify its contents.

Adv Arendse noted that Minister Mchunu had filed a replying affidavit after the close of evidence. Although it did not form part of the evidentiary record considered by the evidence leaders, it had been attached to the report for the Committee's consideration.

In conclusion, Adv Arendse stated that the evidentiary record disclosed a serious and multi-layered institutional crisis. Based on the testimony of 28 witnesses, the evidence leaders had reached several central findings.

Firstly, the directive of 31 December 2024 disbanding the PKTT had been issued without consultation with the National Commissioner, the President, the NPA, the SSA or the Civilian Secretariat. It had been transmitted to a subordinate official rather than to the National Commissioner, while the latter was on leave and on the eve of a public holiday. The reasons later advanced for the directive had not been contemporaneous and had been contradicted by Minister Mchunu's own concessions. Whether the directive had been ultra vires the Minister's powers under sections 206 and 207 of the Constitution remained a matter for determination by the Committee.

Secondly, a credible evidentiary basis existed for the inference that the disbandment had been influenced by the PKTT's success in assisting the Gauteng Counter-Intelligence Operation to expose a criminal syndicate involving law enforcement officials, rather than by the operational or budgetary reasons advanced after 31 December 2024.

Thirdly, the evidence revealed serious corruption in the procurement of the R360 million SAPS healthcare contract, including fronting, tampered bid documents, conflicts of interest, and inadequate due diligence. Adv Arendse noted that the contract had since been cancelled and declared irregular.

Fourthly, Mr Matlala had admitted to making R500 000 in cash payments to a former Minister of Police, engaging in fronting in relation to a major government contract, and concealing his association with a known narcotics manufacturer. Adv Arendse remarked that the individual concerned had reportedly been kidnapped and remained missing. He stated that these admissions independently justified continued criminal investigation and prosecution.

Fifthly, the evidence documented systemic governance failures across SAPS, Crime Intelligence, the NPA, and the Executive. These failures included the politicisation of senior appointments, misuse of the Secret Service Account, inadequate vetting of senior officials, and insufficient external oversight of IDAC prior to October 2025.

Lastly, Adv Arendse stated that the evidence established that the President had not sanctioned the disbandment of the PKTT. His subsequent response, which included establishing the Madlanga Commission, placing the Minister on special leave, and appointing an Acting Minister in terms of section 98 of the Constitution, had been constitutionally grounded. However, the evidence also revealed a pattern of passive executive oversight in the policing portfolio, which the Committee might wish to address in its recommendations.

Adv Bongiwe Mkhize, Junior Counsel, indicated that she had nothing further to add to the presentation.

Adv Arendse stated that an executive summary would be provided to Members, if it had not already been distributed, to assist with the reading and consideration of what was a substantial report. He noted that the matters highlighted during the presentation were the principal issues contained in the report.

The Chairperson thanked Adv Arendse and his team for the presentation. He observed that the report was voluminous but stated that the executive summary would assist Members in understanding the report, its contents and the recommendations being made. He indicated that he would now afford parties an opportunity to raise any questions arising from the presentation before the Committee discussed the way forward.

Discussion

ANC

Ms Sangoni thanked Adv Arendse, Adv Mokhaoetsi, Adv Mkhize and Adv Zikalala for the work that had gone into preparing the report. She stated that the ANC did not have any questions at that stage. She noted that Members would first need to study the report and added that, based on Adv Arendse's presentation, the contents appeared generally straightforward.

MKP

Mr D Skosana (MKP) stated that the report appeared comprehensive and that the party had no objections at that stage. He indicated that the MKP would first consider the documentation in detail before expressing any further views. He commended Adv Arendse and the team for what he described as a comprehensive report.

DA

Adv G Breytenbach (DA) thanked the team for the presentation and described the report as thorough. She indicated that she had no questions and was not aware of any questions from her colleagues.

PA

Mr A Sauls (PA) thanked the team for the presentation. He stated that, on the face of it, there were matters that the party would wish to examine more closely. However, the PA would await the deliberative sessions, during which Members would engage with the report in greater detail and provide their contributions and views. For the present, the party took note of the report.

FF+

Mr W Wessels (FF+) thanked Adv Arendse and the team for both the report and the presentation of the key issues. He stated that he had no questions at that stage and expressed the view that the report would assist the Committee in finalising its work. He added that the FF+ would engage further during deliberations.

ActionSA

Ms D James (ActionSA) thanked Adv Arendse and his team for encapsulating a year's worth of work in the report. She indicated that ActionSA had no questions at that stage but noted that questions might arise at a later point.

IFP

Mr M Mncwango (IFP) apologised for joining the meeting after it had commenced, explaining that he had been travelling from Cape Town to Durban. He stated that he had nevertheless been able to follow most of the presentation. Mr Mncwango joined other Members in commending the legal team for what he described as sterling work in compiling the report. He indicated that the IFP would carefully study the report, examine the key issues raised therein and make its views known during the deliberative process. At that stage, the IFP had no questions.

The Chairperson observed that Members had conveyed two clear messages. Firstly, all parties had congratulated the evidence leaders on the work undertaken. Secondly, Members had unanimously indicated that they required time to study the report before engaging in deliberations and making their reflections known. He therefore invited parties to indicate how much time they required to consider the report.

ANC

Ms Sangoni noted that the report was extensive and that the Committee was mindful of the extension period that had been granted to the Ad Hoc Committee. She proposed that Members be afforded the entirety of the following week to study the report and that deliberations commence during the week beginning 8 June 2026.

MKP

Mr Skosana indicated that he had experienced network difficulties and had not fully heard the discussion.

The Chairperson informed him that Ms Sangoni had proposed that Members be given until the following Friday to study the report and asked whether he had heard the proposal.

Mr Skosana confirmed that he had understood the proposal to allow Members until the following Friday to study the report and stated that the MKP supported it.

The Chairperson clarified that the proposal referred to the following Friday, not the next day.

Mr Skosana confirmed his understanding and reiterated the MKP's support.

The Chairperson then asked whether Mr Skosana supported the proposal.

Mr Shongwe confirmed the MKP's support.

DA

Adv Breytenbach indicated that the DA supported the proposal.

IFP

Mr Mncwango confirmed that the IFP supported the proposal.

PA

Mr Sauls stated that the PA supported the proposal.

ActionSA

Ms James confirmed that ActionSA supported the proposal.

The Chairperson stated that the Committee now had one week within which to study the report. He indicated that he wished to be guided by Members in relation to the approach that would be adopted when the Committee reconvened to consider the report, particularly as to whether the Committee should proceed witness by witness.

He noted that this was not a matter that could be resolved immediately and requested Members to reflect on it so that the Committee could return to the next meeting with a structured approach. He emphasised that it would not be practical for the Committee to approach the report in an unstructured manner or to “run all over the report” without a systematic method of engagement.

The Chairperson suggested that the Committee could, if agreeable, hold a short preparatory meeting, approximately 30 minutes to one hour, either later in the week or at the beginning of the following week, in order to agree on the methodology for considering the report.

He explained that one possible approach would be to consider the report witness by witness. In this regard, he referred to the manner in which the evidence leaders had structured their presentation, where each witness (for example, Lieutenant General Mkhwanazi) had been dealt with individually, including a summary of their evidence, the findings of the evidence leaders, and the corresponding recommendations.

The Chairperson further noted that some witnesses had provided extensive evidence spanning two to four days, and that it might therefore be necessary for the Committee to allocate substantial time, potentially a full day or more, to properly consider such witnesses’ evidence, findings, and recommendations.

He also indicated that alternative approaches could include considering the report page by page, chapter by chapter, or paragraph by paragraph. He stated that, in theory, it might even be possible to complete the report within one or two days, depending on the method adopted, but emphasised that the Committee needed to agree in advance on a structured and consistent approach.

The Chairperson acknowledged that some witnesses, particularly those who had appeared at Kgosi Mampuru in Tshwane during the Committee’s visit to Gauteng, had given relatively short statements that could be dealt with more expeditiously, possibly within one or two pages. However, he stressed that other evidence would require more extensive consideration.

He stated that he did not wish to overburden Members at that stage and therefore encouraged them to use the week to reflect carefully on the most appropriate methodology. He requested Members to consider an approach that would ensure due diligence and procedural fairness in the consideration of the report.

He then asked whether this approach was agreeable to Members.

ANC

Ms Sangoni confirmed that the proposal was agreeable.

MKP

Mr Skosana stated that the MKP supported and agreed with the proposal.

DA

Adv G Breytenbach indicated agreement.

IFP

Mr M Mncwango confirmed agreement.

PA

Mr A Sauls indicated agreement.

FF+

Mr W Wessels stated that the FF Plus agreed.

ActionSA

Ms James confirmed agreement.

The Chairperson requested Mr Ramaano to identify a suitable date and time for a short meeting at which Members could agree on the methodology for considering the report. He noted that Members would also engage informally across party lines in the interim, which would assist in streamlining the Committee’s process when it reconvened.

He then asked Mr Tetyana whether he had anything to add.

Mr Tetyana indicated that he had no further comment.

The Chairperson then invited any final remarks from Adv Arendse or Adv Mkhize.

Adv Arendse thanked the Members and wished them well in their deliberations on the report. He added that the evidence leaders remained available to assist the Committee should the need arise.

The Chairperson asked when the executive summary would be made available.

Adv Arendse replied that the executive summary had already been circulated and, to his understanding, had been forwarded by Adv Mkhize to Mr Ramaano and Mr Tetyana for distribution to Members.

The Chairperson then asked Ms van Zyl-Gous whether she wished to make any remarks.

Ms Nicolette van Zyl-Gous, Committee Content Advisor, indicated that she had no further comments.

The Chairperson asked whether she wished to remind Members of the two reports previously circulated to assist them in conjunction with the evidence leaders’ presentation.

Ms van Zyl-Gous confirmed that two documents had been provided: a governance concerns report and the Committee’s draft report, which would ultimately be tabled in the National Assembly. She clarified that the draft report would be considered after the Committee had concluded its consideration of the evidence leaders’ report.

The Chairperson confirmed that the process would proceed in two phases: first, the evidentiary overview report, and thereafter the Committee’s draft report. Ms van Zyl-Gous confirmed that this was correct.

The Chairperson further noted that an earlier report prepared by the legal team had also been circulated to assist Members in refreshing their recollection and understanding of the evidence.

Ms van Zyl-Gous confirmed that this was correct.

The Chairperson asked whether the legal team had any further input.

Mr Andile Tetyana thanked the Chairperson and indicated that there was nothing further to add.

The Chairperson stated that Members might have observed during the process that certain matters could be relevant to the review of the Rules of Parliament. He further noted that, even during the debate on the Budget Vote in the National Assembly, the Speaker had indicated that, should a process be initiated by the Joint Standing Committee of Parliament and the Office of the Speaker to review the Rules, the Committee should ensure that it did not “miss the boat”.

The Chairperson thanked Members for their participation and indicated that Mr Ramaano would reconvene the Committee at an appropriate time, where Members would agree on the approach to be adopted and proceed with what he referred to as the final phase towards the adoption of the report.

He further thanked Members for their patience and recorded appreciation for Adv Arendse and his team. He stated that any delays that may have occurred were not regarded as deliberate and expressed the view that the evidence leaders would not have intentionally delayed the work of the Committee.

He also referred to comments made by Adv Breytenbach, who had repeatedly stated that the process was “overambitious” and that there was a substantial volume of work to be completed. She had further indicated that the outcome would demonstrate that her assessment had been correct.

The Chairperson concluded by expressing his gratitude to all Members of the Committee, noting that the Committee had reached its current stage through cooperation and collective effort, and invoking the expression that “one swallow does not make a summer”.

Mr Shongwe informed the Committee that, for the record, he had been invited to appear before the Madlanga Commission and had subsequently provided a statement as requested in relation to the matter concerning General Khan and the alleged invitation extended to him in Durban. He stated that he wished to place this information on record for the attention of the Chairperson and the Committee.

The Chairperson thanked Mr Shongwe for the information and extended well wishes to him, encouraging him to testify with courage and in the best interests of the country.

Mr Skosana expressed appreciation for the Chairperson’s remarks and conveyed support to his colleague. He stated that, in his view, the arrest of General Khan had vindicated concerns previously raised by Members, and reiterated that individuals who wished to engage the Committee should appear before it formally, as had been the practice of the Ad Hoc Committee.

He further remarked that lessons should be drawn from the matter and suggested that proposals be made in due course to the Speaker regarding the possibility of future inquiries, including matters relating to Correctional Services and alleged corruption within that department, as well as issues concerning the judiciary, should they arise.

The Chairperson then asked Mr Sauls whether he wished to offer a brief closing prayer before the meeting was adjourned.

Mr Sauls indicated that he would do so. Before praying, he wished Mr Shongwe well and advised him to remain composed. He then led the Committee in prayer, giving thanks for the work undertaken, expressing gratitude for the opportunity to serve, and praying for wisdom, integrity, and guidance in the completion of the Committee’s work. He further prayed that the Committee’s work would contribute to the betterment of the country and serve as a legacy for future generations.

The meeting was adjourned.
