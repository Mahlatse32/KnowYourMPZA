KZN, EC & WC DHA Performance & operations; DPWI on DHA Office conditions and space (Deputy Minister)

Home Affairs

10 March 2026
Chairperson: Mr M Chabane (ANC)
Share this page:

Meeting Summary

Video

The Portfolio Committee on Home Affairs convened to receive briefings from the Department of Home Affairs (DHA) and the Department of Public Works and Infrastructure (DPWI) on the state of DHA-occupied facilities in the Eastern Cape, KwaZulu-Natal and Western Cape.

In addition, the Committee also deliberated on matters arising from prior correspondence to the Minister of Home Affairs, including the digitisation programme, immigration oversight, and allegations concerning the Director-General.

The Deputy Minister of Home Affairs outlined that the digitisation initiative forms part of the Presidential Employment Stimulus Programme, aimed at converting paper-based civic records into digital formats while creating temporary employment for graduates. Contracts for workers engaged in the project have been extended to March 2026, pending the finalisation of procurement processes for a new service provider.

Committee Members raised concerns regarding labour conditions, underscoring the Department’s responsibility to ensure fair and lawful treatment of workers employed through third-party contractors. Members further queried reports involving individuals allegedly linked to a right-wing group who had travelled to South Africa. The Director-General confirmed that the Department is working in coordination with the Department of International Relations and Cooperation to verify whether such individuals entered the country and to assess potential contraventions of section 29 of the Immigration Act.

Provincial managers from KwaZulu-Natal, the Western Cape and the Eastern Cape reported on operational performance, service delivery pressures, infrastructure limitations and staffing capacity. KwaZulu-Natal highlighted acute personnel shortages, with only 647 officials in post against an approved establishment of 1,966. Notwithstanding these constraints, the province exceeded its targets for Smart ID card applications and issuances, although infrastructure deficiencies, fleet management costs and broader financial pressures remain significant impediments.

The DPWI acknowledged that a number of DHA offices are housed in substandard or unsuitable facilities. Persistent delays in procurement processes, coupled with escalating rental costs, have exacerbated infrastructure challenges. The Department confirmed that DHA operates from a mix of leased and state-owned properties, and that steps are being taken to identify alternative accommodation and strengthen compliance with the Government Immovable Asset Management Act.

Committee Members expressed strong concern regarding deteriorating infrastructure, persistent long queues, staffing shortages and system downtime, all of which continue to undermine service delivery. The Committee stressed that safe and well-maintained facilities are critical for both employees and the public, and criticised DPWI for slow progress in resolving infrastructure deficiencies. Members also called for definitive timelines on the investigation into allegations against the Director-General, and requested further details on deportation statistics and immigration enforcement measures.

Meeting report

Opening remarks 

The Chairperson noted that the Committee had previously engaged with the Department of Public Works and Infrastructure and had held productive discussions during that engagement. The current meeting formed part of the Committee’s continued oversight engagements with the Department of Home Affairs, particularly with provincial structures.

The Chairperson explained that the Committee had previously engaged with the Ministry and the Director-General at the national level. However, the Committee considered it important to engage directly with provincial structures better to understand operational challenges at the service delivery level. He noted that the Committee had identified several issues during public hearings and through concerns raised by Members of Parliament, members of provincial legislatures, councillors, and members of the public regarding the services provided by the Department of Home Affairs in their respective areas. The Committee, therefore, wanted to ensure that information provided at the national level corresponded with realities on the ground.

He indicated that the Committee had begun similar engagements with Mpumalanga and Limpopo previously, which had resulted in constructive discussions. The Director-General had already noted some of the concerns raised during those engagements. The Chairperson emphasised that provincial leadership should use the opportunity to raise both challenges and progress with the Committee. He encouraged officials to be open and frank during the engagement, stressing that the Committee was providing a platform for provincial managers to interact with Members directly.

The Chairperson acknowledged that senior leadership from the Department, including the Deputy Minister, Director-General and Deputy Directors-General, were present. He urged provincial officials not to hesitate to raise concerns even in the presence of senior leadership, as the Committee sought to collectively respond to the expectations of South Africans. Members of Parliament were responsible for conducting oversight, while the Department was responsible for delivering services to communities.

He noted that South Africans had raised several concerns regarding the Department’s services, including system downtime, infrastructure challenges, under-staffing, and matters arising from the interim findings of the Special Investigating Unit (SIU) report, which had been under consideration since the Sixth Parliament. While there were challenges, the Committee also wished to acknowledge the dedication of officials who upheld the Batho Pele principles and worked diligently to deliver services in line with constitutional obligations.

The Chairperson commended the Director-General for consistently attending Committee meetings, even when difficult issues were under discussion. He noted that the DG had demonstrated accountability by appearing before the Committee and engaging with Members. This, he said, was an important example of leadership and transparency.

He further explained that when the Committee wrote correspondence to the Department, it was often based on issues raised by members of the public through their elected representatives. The Committee therefore regarded such matters as legitimate issues requiring engagement with the executive authority, including the Minister, Deputy Minister and Director-General. The Chairperson emphasised that while parliamentary questions remained one mechanism for raising issues in Parliament, the Portfolio Committee also had the authority to engage directly with the Department on matters brought before it.

The Chairperson clarified that the Committee would not adopt a practice whereby matters raised through Committee correspondence were redirected solely to parliamentary questions. Instead, issues brought to the Committee’s attention would continue to be addressed through oversight engagements with the Department.

He outlined that the Committee had a structured programme of engagements with provincial offices. Once all provinces had been briefed, the Committee would assess the information received and develop a report with recommendations on how to strengthen oversight and service delivery within the Department of Home Affairs.

The Chairperson also indicated that the Committee planned to invite the Department’s information technology (IT) leadership to a future meeting to brief Members on the status of the Department’s IT environment. He emphasised that the Committee was aware of the challenges within the IT system and wanted a dedicated engagement to ensure improvements in service delivery.

Regarding the meeting proceedings, the Chairperson requested Members to raise questions directly after each provincial presentation to allow for focused engagement and efficient use of time. The Committee had been allocated time until 15:00, and an extension had been requested to allow for a thorough discussion.

Mr Livhuwani Tommy Makhode, DG, DHA, introduced the provincial management teams from KwaZulu-Natal, the Eastern Cape and the Western Cape, and other officials who were present to brief the Committee.

The Chairperson invited the Committee Secretary to outline the issues contained in the correspondence.

Mr Eddie Mathonsi, Committee Secretary, informed the Committee that several letters had been sent to the Minister. One of the matters raised concerned the digitalisation project, particularly complaints from staff members working on the project regarding the status of their contracts. He explained that some of the contracts had expired in December and had subsequently been extended until the end of March, which had resulted in concerns from employees about the terms and continuation of their contracts.

Another matter related to Homestead Park in Johannesburg, where the Department had conducted an operation on 18 November of the previous year. Concerns had been raised about allegations that undocumented foreign nationals were being employed at the site. The request had been made for the Department of Home Affairs, together with the Department of Employment and Labour, to revisit the area to ensure compliance with immigration and labour regulations.

The Committee Secretary further noted that the Committee had requested a response from the Department regarding allegations that had been made against the Director-General. In addition, a letter had been received from a Member of Parliament requesting clarification on the application of Section 29 of the Immigration Act in relation to individuals who had travelled from the United States to South Africa.

He added that another matter concerned the Home Affairs office in Atlantis, which the Chairperson had previously visited. A response from the Department had also been requested on issues relating to that office.

The Committee Secretary concluded by indicating that the Department would be invited to provide an update and responses on the matters raised in the correspondence.

Deputy Minister

Mr Njabulo Nzuza, Deputy Minister of Home Affairs, responded to the matters raised under the Committee’s correspondence. He indicated that while members of the departmental team would provide more detailed responses, he would address the issues at a high level.

On the matter of the digitalisation project, the Deputy Minister explained that the Department had previously recruited staff as part of the Presidential Employment Stimulus Programme to assist with the digitisation of Home Affairs records. These employees had been appointed on three-year contracts, which corresponded with the anticipated duration of the project and the availability of funding at the time. As a result, the contracts had reached their conclusion after three years.

However, the Deputy Minister confirmed that the Department was currently working on extending those contracts. He noted that the Department had previously granted short-term extensions to some of the staff while aligning the process with available resources. Additional funding had since been allocated in the current financial year to continue supporting the project. As a result, the Department was exploring options to extend the contracts. However, the extension model might differ from the previous arrangement due to capacity constraints within the Department related to recruitment processes. He added that senior officials would provide further details on the digitalisation project.

About the Homestead Park matter in Johannesburg, which related to allegations that undocumented foreign nationals were being employed in the area, the Deputy Minister indicated that the Department had already provided a response outlining the work being undertaken to address the issue.

On the matter concerning allegations made against the Director-General, the Deputy Minister informed the Committee that the Department had appointed a law firm to investigate the allegations. Once the investigation had been concluded, the findings would be presented to the Portfolio Committee.

Regarding the remaining matter relating to questions raised in member statements, the Deputy Minister indicated that departmental officials would provide further clarification where necessary.

He concluded by indicating that the relevant officials would provide additional details on the digitalisation project. At the same time, the Director-General would elaborate further on the remaining matters raised by the Committee.

Mr Thulani Mavuso, DDG: Institutional Planning and Support, DHA, elaborated on the matter relating to the digitisation project. He explained that when the project was conceptualised, the Department recognised that it still held a large number of paper-based records, which posed challenges for the Department’s broader digital transformation agenda. The digitisation of these records was therefore necessary to ensure that documents could be easily retrieved when required for services such as personal amendments, investigations and verification processes.

He noted that in some cases, individuals possessed identity documents, but when the Department attempted to verify their information, the corresponding birth records were not readily available in the system. Digitising historical records would therefore ensure that officials could access documents more efficiently when conducting investigations or processing applications.

He explained that the project formed part of the Department’s broader digital transformation programme, which aimed to convert paper-based records into digital formats. At the same time, the project had also been designed to contribute to addressing youth unemployment by providing unemployed graduates with an opportunity to obtain practical workplace experience and develop skills that would improve their employability.

He emphasised that the initiative had not been intended to provide permanent employment for the staff members recruited under the programme. The Department did not have permanent positions available for these individuals because the purpose of the project was to offer temporary work experience rather than long-term employment.

He informed the Committee that the contracts of the project staff had expired in the previous year and had subsequently been extended in October until 31 March 2026. The extension had been aligned with the financial year and with the Department’s Annual Performance Plan (APP) targets, which included targets related to the digitisation project.

He further explained that the Department had approached the market in November to appoint a new service provider to support the continuation of the project, as the contract of the current service provider was due to expire at the end of January. However, the procurement process had not been successful because only one bid submission had been received under the SETA Request for Proposal (RFP) framework, which the Department had used to source service providers for the work.

As a result, the Department had decided to redraft the specifications and return to the market. In the interim, the Department had extended the contract of the current service provider for six months to ensure that the project could continue without interruption.

Looking ahead to the new financial year beginning on 1 April, the Department intended to maintain continuity while the procurement process for a new service provider was finalised. The DG indicated that the Department had requested the current service provider to retain the remaining cohort of approximately 1 725 workers so that they could continue working until the end of July 2026.

He explained that once a new service provider had been appointed, two possible options would be considered. The first option would be for the existing workers to continue working under the newly appointed service provider, while the second option would involve the service provider augmenting capacity by increasing the number of workers participating in the project.

He also addressed a concern that had been raised in a letter submitted in December regarding the temporary closure of operations during the December period. He explained that the Department’s initial intention had been to close production activities during that period to allow staff to take leave. However, concerns had been raised by some workers who preferred to continue working. Following discussions, the Department had decided not to close the facility, allowing those who wished to continue working to do so while others proceeded on leave.

He concluded that the matter had therefore been resolved internally before it was raised with the Committee.

Mr Makhode responded to a matter raised in correspondence from a Member of Parliament concerning a group referred to as the Young Republicans of New York, which had allegedly made racist and anti-Semitic comments or engaged in related activities.

The DG explained that the Department had received correspondence regarding the matter; however, the information provided did not include specific details about the individuals involved. As a result, the Department was currently working with the Department of International Relations and Cooperation (DIRCO) and internal departmental teams to identify whether any individuals linked to the group had connections to South Africa.

He noted that the Department was examining whether any such individuals might be linked to the country through visa applications, asylum processes, or any other immigration-related activities. This process would assist the Department in determining whether any persons associated with the matter were present in South Africa or had engaged with the country’s immigration system.

The DG stated that once further details had been identified, the Department would report back to the Committee. The investigation would also determine whether there had been any violation of Section 29 of the Immigration Act, which relates to the prohibition of certain persons entering or remaining in the Republic. He concluded that the Department would provide further feedback to the Committee once the necessary information had been obtained.

Discussion

Mr A Roos (DA) commented on the matter relating to the digitisation project. He noted that the project had originally been initiated as part of a Presidential initiative and was intended to be a once-off intervention rather than a permanent programme.

He stated that the initiative had not initially been a project of the Department of Home Affairs, but rather a Presidential Employment Stimulus initiative designed to run for a limited period. He acknowledged that the Department had subsequently attempted to extend the project within the available budget, in order to continue the work for as long as possible.

Mr Roos emphasised that it was important to recognise the original nature of the programme, noting that it had not been intended to create permanent employment opportunities. As such, there should not be an expectation that the initiative would result in permanent employment for the individuals involved.

He concluded that the Department had made efforts to continue the project within its resource constraints, but the original design of the programme had been temporary in nature.

Mr T Mogale (EFF) noted the Department’s response regarding matters related to the digitisation project, but indicated that workers involved in the project had subsequently contacted him. He stated that the workers had informed him that they had engaged with Mr Thulani Mavuso, Deputy Director-General (DDG): Institutional Planning and Support, and had raised concerns relating to their employment conditions.

Mr Mogale explained that the workers were employed through a private company contracted to undertake the digitisation work and that they had raised labour-related concerns regarding their treatment and working conditions. He emphasised that the Department, as the contracting authority for the company, should not ignore such concerns on the basis that a third-party service provider employed the workers. He argued that it would be the Department’s responsibility to ensure that labour issues raised by workers were appropriately addressed.

He further informed the Committee that the workers had requested a meeting with him and that he intended to engage with them in order to obtain a clearer understanding of the issues they had raised.

Mr Mogale also referred to the letter that the Committee had written to the Minister of Home Affairs, and expressed appreciation for the Chairperson’s earlier remarks on the matter. He criticised the response received from the Minister, describing it as dismissive and inappropriate, and argued that Members of Parliament had a responsibility to raise issues on behalf of the public.

He emphasised that when Members of Parliament raised matters with the Minister, whether through letters or parliamentary processes, the Minister had a duty to respond. He stated that Members could not be instructed to rely solely on parliamentary questions when raising issues of concern.

Mr Mogale further explained that the matter raised in the Committee’s correspondence related to concerns about individuals associated with right-wing groups internationally who had recently travelled to South Africa. He stated that some of these individuals allegedly held racist and anti-Semitic views and were reportedly using social media platforms to promote narratives such as claims of a “white genocide” in South Africa.

He argued that such developments raised concerns regarding immigration control and vetting processes at ports of entry, and whether individuals entering the country were being adequately screened.

Referring to Section 29 of the Immigration Act, Mr Mogale noted that individuals who were associated with organisations that promoted racist or anti-Semitic views could be prohibited from entering the Republic. He therefore welcomed the response provided by the Director-General and expressed the expectation that the Committee would receive a detailed report once the Department had completed its investigations into the matter.

Ms H Mkhaliphi (EFF) indicated that she would not speak at length as some of the issues she had intended to raise had already been addressed by her colleague, with whom she agreed.

She noted, however, that during the Director-General’s earlier response, the audio had not been very clear, possibly due to microphone issues. While acknowledging the response already provided by the DG, she requested clarity on the timeframe for addressing the matter raised in the Committee’s correspondence. She stated that when a department receives correspondence from a Member of Parliament and finds that the information provided is insufficient, the Department should respond proactively to request further details rather than waiting to raise the issue during a Committee meeting.

Ms Mkhaliphi emphasised that matters raised by Members of Parliament were often based on concerns from communities, and therefore required prompt and proactive engagement by the Department.

Turning to the digitisation project, she addressed remarks made earlier suggesting that there should be no expectation of employment arising from the project. She disagreed with this view, noting that South Africa faced a high level of unemployment, estimated at approximately 9.8 million people, particularly affecting young people. She argued that Members of Parliament could not disregard the expectations of unemployed youth who participated in such programmes and hoped that these opportunities might lead to continued employment.

She further requested clarity from the Deputy Director-General responsible for the project regarding the Department’s broader plan for digital transformation. She noted that the digitisation of Home Affairs processes was long overdue, citing examples of large volumes of manual applications that were still being processed, including situations where South Africans abroad who lost their passports had to rely on paper-based application systems.

Ms Mkhaliphi emphasised that the Department needed to move away from manual systems and accelerate the transition to digital services. She therefore requested that the Department outline its long-term strategy and plan for digitisation, beyond the initial project introduced under the Presidential initiative.

She concluded by stating that the Committee expected clarity on how the Department intended to modernise its systems and fully implement digital processes, as reliance on manual systems was no longer sustainable.

Mr S Ngubane (MKP) referred to the matter concerning the allegations made against the Director-General and noted the response provided by the Deputy Minister. He requested further clarity regarding the timeline of the investigation, specifically when the law firm had been appointed to investigate the matter and how far the investigative process had progressed. He stated that this information would assist the Committee in understanding the progress being made and when it could expect the findings of the investigation.

Mr Ngubane also expressed concern regarding the response received from the Minister to the Committee’s correspondence. He supported the earlier remarks made by Mr Mogale (EFF), noting that Members had written to the Minister because the matter raised was of concern. In his view, the Minister should have responded directly to the issues raised in the letter.

He further stated that it was concerning that the response had suggested that Members should instead raise the matter through parliamentary questions. He argued that Members of Parliament had the right to raise matters with the executive through correspondence and that the Minister should provide direct responses to such issues rather than instructing Members on how to frame or submit their questions.

Mr Ngubane concluded by emphasising that the Minister should provide clear responses when concerns were raised by Members acting on behalf of the public.

Addressing the question raised by Mr Ngubane regarding the investigation into allegations against the Director-General, the Deputy Minister indicated that the Department would provide a written response to the Committee outlining the relevant timelines. He explained that the Department would first need to engage with the law firm appointed to investigate the matter in order to determine the expected timeframe for the completion of the investigation. He stated that it would therefore be more appropriate to submit a detailed response to the Committee once the Department had consulted with the appointed investigators.

Turning to the digitisation project, the Deputy Minister emphasised that the Department remained committed to ensuring that the project reached its intended conclusion. He explained that the project involved the digitisation of more than 350 million records, which was necessary to support the Department’s broader digital transformation and improve the accessibility of historical records.

He noted that the Department had experienced challenges during the implementation of the project, particularly due to delays in the allocation of funding. As a result, the Department had required time to develop the necessary infrastructure to support the digitisation process. The Deputy Minister stated that the main constraint affecting the project had been the availability of financial resources.

Despite these challenges, the Department had attempted to sustain the project by utilising its own internal budget resources to continue employing the staff recruited for the initiative. He informed the Committee that the Department had recently received additional funding, which would assist in continuing the project during the current financial year. However, he cautioned that the funding received was limited to the current financial year and did not cover future years.

The Deputy Minister added that it would have been irresponsible for the Department to continue recruiting staff on long-term contracts without certainty regarding the availability of funding. For this reason, the Department had been cautious in extending the employment arrangements for those participating in the project.

He further indicated that the digitisation programme represented one of the largest digitisation projects on the African continent, involving the conversion of hundreds of millions of paper records into digital formats. In light of the scale and significance of the initiative, he suggested that the Department could arrange a dedicated briefing to the Committee to provide a more detailed presentation on the project, including the progress made and the expected impact on the Department’s transition towards paperless records management.

The Deputy Minister also noted that the Department was making progress in developing digital service platforms. As part of these efforts, the Department had opened additional service points in certain international locations to assist South Africans abroad and improve the efficiency with which documents were processed and delivered.

He concluded by stating that the Department would return to the Committee with responses on the remaining matters raised during the discussion and would continue engaging with the Committee on the Department’s digital transformation initiatives.

The Chairperson proposed that the Committee schedule a dedicated engagement with the Director-General, the State Information Technology Agency (SITA), and the Department’s Information Technology (IT) team to receive a comprehensive report on the Department’s digital systems and related matters.

He indicated that the Committee would arrange the meeting in the near future so that Members could engage in detail with the issues raised. The Chairperson emphasised that the Committee would require clear timeframes on the matters raised during the discussion, including the allegations that had been raised against the Director-General, as well as the issues contained in the correspondence previously sent by the Committee to the Ministry.

The Chairperson noted that the item had assisted the Committee in gaining a better understanding of the Department’s current position and would also help Members respond to concerns raised by their constituencies.

He indicated that further engagement would take place during the planned meeting with the Department’s IT leadership and SITA.

The Chairperson then invited the Deputy Minister to introduce the three provincial reports, as well as the briefing from the Department of Public Works and Infrastructure.

The Deputy Minister indicated that the briefing formed part of the continuation of the Committee’s oversight engagements with the Department, which had commenced in the previous week. These engagements followed interactions between the Portfolio Committee and the Department after the Committee visited various provinces.

He explained that the purpose of the current session was to provide the Committee with updates on the operations and performance of the Department’s provincial offices in KwaZulu-Natal, the Western Cape and the Eastern Cape.

The Deputy Minister noted that the provincial managers from the respective provinces were present to lead the presentations and would brief the Committee on the operational matters and service delivery issues within their provinces.

He further indicated that the Director-General had already introduced the provincial teams, and the officials would proceed with their presentations in accordance with the order reflected on the Committee’s agenda.

DHA KwaZulu-Natal Presentation

Mr Cyril Mncwabe, Provincial Manager: KZN, DHA, informed the Committee that he would lead the presentation on the operations and performance of the Department’s KwaZulu-Natal provincial office.

He indicated that the presentation would begin with a high-level overview of the province, including a map outlining the provincial and district demarcations. The presentation would also cover the allocation of mobile units, service areas within the province, and the organisational and governance structures of the provincial office.

Mr Mncwabe further noted that the briefing would address stakeholder relations and provide an overview of the human resource capacity within the province, including staffing levels across districts, priority posts, and progress made by the staffing committee in addressing vacancies.

In addition, the presentation would include updates on investigations conducted by the counter-corruption unit, as well as progress on labour relations matters within the province.

He added that the briefing would also cover the provincial budget allocation and expenditure, including financial pressures affecting the province, as well as matters relating to fleet management and property infrastructure used by the Department in KwaZulu-Natal.

Mr Mncwabe briefed the Committee on the operations and performance of the provincial office. He began by outlining the provincial service footprint, which consists of 155 service points, including 57 local offices, 33 mobile units, 62 health facilities, three bank branches and one mall office. The province has an approved establishment of 1 966 posts, but only 647 officials are currently in place, leaving significant vacancies. He noted that some priority posts, including office manager positions, were in the process of being filled through the staffing committee.

He explained the organisational and governance structures within the province, including the provincial management structure, district management arrangements aligned with municipal demarcations, and governance forums such as provincial executive management meetings, provincial management meetings, budget committees and consultative forums with labour unions. The province also participates in various stakeholder coordination structures, including the Justice, Crime Prevention and Security (JCPS) cluster, provincial social cluster forums and engagements with the Independent Electoral Commission.

Mr Mncwabe further outlined the human resource capacity across districts, highlighting staffing shortages at several offices and health facilities responsible for birth registration. He indicated that some health facilities had limited or no permanent staff assigned, which affected the timely registration of births. Updates were also provided on counter-corruption investigations and labour relations matters, with several cases finalised while others remained under investigation or disciplinary processes.

In terms of financial management, he reported that the provincial budget amounted to approximately R350 million, with 83% already spent. Budget pressures were largely related to fuel, fleet maintenance, and operational costs, particularly following the allocation of additional vehicles to the province without corresponding budget adjustments. He also highlighted pressures linked to limited staff capacity, asset management requirements, and infrastructure constraints.

Despite these challenges, the province had exceeded its targets for Smart ID card applications and issuances, achieving over 200% of the target for applications and significantly surpassing issuance targets. Progress was also reported in birth registration and late registration of births, although some backlogs remained. In addition, the province had conducted numerous immigration enforcement operations, detected employers contravening immigration legislation, and carried out deportations and transfers of undocumented foreign nationals to the Lindela Repatriation Centre.

Mr Mncwabe also outlined risk governance and mitigation measures, identifying risks such as fraudulent birth registrations, fraudulent Smart ID card applications, and theft of cash collected at offices. To mitigate these risks, the province was promoting birth registration at health facilities, strict adherence to standard operating procedures for Smart ID applications, and the implementation of cashless transactions at service points.

He further highlighted infrastructure challenges affecting service delivery, including inadequate or non-compliant office accommodation in areas such as Pietermaritzburg, Stanger, Ulundi and Bulwer. In some instances, services were being provided from mobile units or temporary facilities due to unsafe or incomplete buildings, and the province continued to engage the Department of Public Works and Infrastructure to secure suitable accommodation.

Mr Mncwabe concluded by noting that three officials had been appointed to manage deportations and transfers to the Lindela Repatriation Centre. However, no additional budget had been allocated to support these operations despite the provision of additional vehicles. The final slide contained provincial contact details for senior officials, office managers and district managers for the Committee’s noting. He thanked the Chairperson and Members and concluded the presentation.

DHA Western Cape Presentation

Mr Yusuf Simons, Provincial Manager: WC, DHA, briefed the Committee on the operations, service footprint, challenges and performance of the provincial office, highlighting developments in the province that affect service delivery.

He began by outlining the provincial context, noting that the Western Cape’s population has grown to more than 7 million, placing increasing pressure on Home Affairs services, including longer queues and rising demand for Smart ID applications and other civic services. The province is divided into five districts and one metro, with the Department represented in 20 of the 24 local municipalities, while mobile units service the remaining areas.

In terms of service infrastructure, the Western Cape has 30 Home Affairs offices, of which 17 provide immigration services. Approximately 90% of the offices are modernised, and with the completion of the Lainsburg office modernisation, the province expects to reach almost full modernisation. Additional service points include one mall office at Tygervalley (booking-based services), a proof-of-concept one-stop service model in Epping, 23 connected hospitals for birth registrations, five participating banks for Smart ID and passport services, and 15 mobile units servicing rural and underserved communities. The province also operates a central provincial archive in Bellville to store documentation removed from offices.

The Provincial Manager highlighted several unique provincial developments and pressures affecting Home Affairs. These include the expansion of the Saldanha industrial hub, which attracts population growth and the establishment of both formal and informal settlements requiring increased departmental presence. The province also experiences frequent fire disasters, which destroy personal documents and require rapid response from the Department. During the financial year, the provincial office responded to 24 fire incidents, providing replacement identity documents and birth certificates to victims free of charge through an established disaster response system.

Another ongoing issue relates to temporary shelters for refugees at Wingfield and Paint City, which were originally established during the COVID-19 period but remain occupied. The Department continues to provide basic services such as sanitation and electricity while working with the City of Cape Town and the Department of Public Works and Infrastructure to obtain eviction orders.

The Provincial Manager also noted that the agricultural sector, particularly in the Cape Metro, West Coast and surrounding areas, attracts large numbers of seasonal migrant workers, especially from Lesotho, many of whom remain after the season. This places pressure on immigration enforcement resources and has required increased operations and deportations.

Regarding governance and management, the provincial office maintains regular management and oversight structures, including executive management meetings, extended management meetings, labour consultative forums with organised labour, and immigration operational committees to coordinate law-enforcement activities and ensure compliance with immigration regulations.

The presentation also highlighted the use of mobile units, which serve numerous remote communities and municipalities where permanent offices are not available. These units provide services such as ID applications, birth registrations and other civic services.

A significant challenge raised was the abuse of the online booking system, where individuals hijack booking slots and resell them to members of the public at high prices. This practice results in double bookings, client frustration and conflict at offices. In response, the Department has identified and removed fraudulent bookings and laid criminal charges against individuals involved. It is working with the IT unit to restrict suspicious IP addresses and review the booking system to prevent abuse.

The Provincial Manager also addressed several operational challenges at specific offices. The Atlantis Home Affairs office was closed after the lease for the building, located in a Thusong Centre, was not renewed due to poor infrastructure conditions, health risks and safety concerns, including gangsterism and intimidation of staff and clients. As an interim measure, the Department provides services through mobile units and birth registration at Wesfleur Hospital, while working to establish a permanent office in a local shopping mall, subject to approval and leasing arrangements through Public Works.

Mr Simons highlighted several outreach and service delivery initiatives undertaken by the province. These include school ID campaigns, late registration of births programmes, Smart ID card campaigns and support to communities affected by fire disasters. He emphasised that the province must remain agile in responding to disasters, particularly where fires destroy residents’ identity documents shortly before registration or voting periods. In such cases, the Department works closely with ward councillors and disaster management structures, while the Director-General is able to approve the waiving of document replacement fees to assist affected communities.

He further outlined the Department’s high-impact outreach programme, which focuses on improving access to services in rural and underserved areas. These outreach initiatives deploy multiple mobile units, often ten or more at a time, to a specific area for approximately five days, providing services such as Smart ID applications, late birth registrations and the issuing of identity documents. Since 2021, several rural areas have been serviced through these campaigns. In the previous year, the province conducted an outreach programme in the Cape Winelands, deploying 13 mobile units (including support from other provinces), which resulted in 3 322 Smart ID applications processed in one week. Officials also voluntarily extended their work during the campaign to continue services over the Women’s Day weekend.

He also addressed the issue of staffing within the Department, indicating that when the Department returns to the Committee, it should present clear timelines for the filling of funded vacancies, including the timeline for advertisements and recruitment processes. While the Committee understood the constraints imposed by the national budget, it emphasised that prioritisation was essential to ensure that critical frontline services are strengthened.

The Chairperson noted that during oversight visits, the Committee had observed situations where staff members were responsible for multiple functions, citing examples from visits to Groblersdal and Mthatha, where difficult working conditions had been observed. He stressed that the Department needed to reprioritise its planning to address these operational pressures.

He further indicated that the Director-General should follow up on issues raised by Ms Jama and Mr Hala, particularly regarding matters affecting the Eastern Cape, which the Committee would continue to monitor closely. Similar follow-up would also be undertaken regarding issues raised in KwaZulu-Natal, as well as other provinces where challenges had been identified.

The Chairperson emphasised that the Committee could not ignore the complaint raised by Mr Ngubane regarding allegations of abuse, bullying, and unsafe working environments for staff members, stating that the Committee would also follow up on that matter.

He informed Members that the Committee would receive a joint briefing from the IEC and the Department of Home Affairs on preparations for the local government elections, including work currently underway to prepare for the electoral process. This briefing would focus on operational readiness beyond the legislative processes already before the Committee.

The Chairperson reiterated that if the Committee did not manage the matter firmly, particularly in relation to Public Works, it risked failing the Department of Home Affairs, which depends on proper infrastructure to deliver services. He encouraged the Director-General to engage with the Director-General of Public Works and the relevant Ministers on possible arrangements for the transfer or shared management of infrastructure responsibilities, particularly in relation to government-owned buildings.

While acknowledging that legislative constraints existed regarding infrastructure management, the Chairperson stressed that mechanisms needed to be explored to strengthen the infrastructure environment supporting Home Affairs services. The Committee could not continuously invite Home Affairs to account for infrastructure issues when many of those issues fell under Public Works.

He further indicated that the Committee would package its recommendations and submit them to the Executive, including the relevant Ministers, as part of its oversight and accountability responsibilities.

The Chairperson also noted that infrastructure challenges had been tracked by the Committee for several years, including during the period when Minister Sputla was responsible for the Infrastructure Unit, and that the Committee had continued to monitor the matter since then.

He raised concerns about unfinished Public Works projects, particularly in North West and Limpopo, where offices had reportedly remained incomplete for several years despite commitments that construction would be finalised. The Committee had even interviewed security personnel stationed at some of these sites during oversight visits, which further highlighted the delays.

The Chairperson therefore emphasised that when the Department returned to brief the Committee, it should present a clear plan on the completion of modernised offices. He stressed that while the Committee supported the Department’s modernisation programme, it was also necessary to balance this with the realities and challenges faced by communities on the ground.

He cautioned that the Department could not focus only on modernisation initiatives. At the same time, conditions at existing offices remained poor, as this would create a disconnect between policy ambitions and service delivery realities.

The Chairperson also referred to the bank partnership programme, noting that while provinces such as the Western Cape appeared to be more advanced in benefiting from this programme, the Department needed to ensure that other provinces also benefit from the rollout. The Committee would therefore require a detailed implementation plan indicating where the programme would be expanded across provinces.

He concluded by thanking Members for their comments and engagement with the report and expressed appreciation for the Department’s participation in the meeting.

The Chairperson reiterated the Committee’s observations regarding areas requiring improvement and emphasised that the Department should address these matters together with the Executive leadership.

The Chairperson then adjourned the meeting.
