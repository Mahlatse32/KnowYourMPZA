CLOSED: DCS Litigation; Inmate classification processes; Gang Management Strategy; with Ministry

Correctional Services

03 March 2026
Chairperson: Ms A Ramolobeng (ANC)
Share this page:

Meeting Summary

The Portfolio Committee on Correctional Services met to consider correspondence and Department presentations on sensitive matters, including the inmate classification process and the gang prevention strategy. After reviewing the rules on open and closed sessions, the Committee determined that the agenda items would be dealt with in a closed session due to the security-sensitive nature of the information.

The Chairperson emphasised that this was the first closed meeting since the Committee's inception and would not become a norm. Minister Pieter Groenewald and the National Commissioner confirmed the sensitivity of the matters, and a summary and recommendations from the closed session would be provided to those requiring a briefing.

Meeting report

Opening Remarks

The Chairperson opened the meeting and welcomed Members, the Department of Correctional Services (DCS) led by the Minister and Deputy Minister, the National Commissioner, and the State Attorney from the Department of Justice. She stated that there had been prior discussions with the Minister, as the Committee had initially requested only the State Attorney's Office to attend when litigation reports were presented.

She referred to the draft agenda, which included opening and welcome, apologies, consideration of correspondence, presentations from the Department, and closing remarks. She indicated that the correspondence received related to item four, which concerned the nature of the Department's presentations.

She further stated that the consideration of correspondence under item three would determine how the meeting would proceed. The meeting was opened to the public pending that determination. She then called for a mover and seconder for the adoption of the agenda to enable the Committee to proceed.

The Chairperson confirmed that the agenda had been duly adopted and noted the apologies received from Members. She indicated that the Committee would proceed to item three, which was the consideration of correspondence.

She stated that Members had received correspondence from the Office of the National Commissioner, as circulated by the Committee Secretary. The communication indicated that the presentations on the inmate classification process and the gang management strategy were sensitive, which was the reason the documents had not been shared in advance. She reminded Members that documents were ordinarily circulated beforehand to allow adequate preparation.

She further stated that the only documents shared related to litigation matters, as these were in the public domain. However, she noted that even in such instances, there could be limitations on discussing the merits and demerits in an open session.

Considering the request received, she stated that the Portfolio Committee needed to determine how the meeting would proceed. She requested that the applicable rules and relevant provisions be outlined to guide Members in their deliberations before deciding whether the meeting should remain open or be closed, given the sensitivity of the information to be presented.

Discussion

The Committee Secretary outlined the rules on opening and closing Committee meetings to the public. He noted that Rule 184 of the National Assembly Rules generally allowed public access but permitted the Chairperson to exclude the public under certain circumstances. Specifically, Rule 184(2)(a) and (b) required the Committee to consider any decision to exclude the public, while the Chairperson could rule a meeting closed before it started, and the Committee could decide to open or close the meeting at any time. He concluded that the Committee needed to deliberate on the correspondence from the Department in line with the rule.

The Chairperson noted that the Committee was supported by Parliament’s legal team for guidance on litigation matters. She reminded Members that if the meeting remained open, the Department could not present on the inmate classification process and gang management strategy due to sensitivity. She invited Members to discuss how the meeting should proceed.

Mr C Niehaus (EFF) stated that closing the meeting was advisable for the litigation matters and inmate classification process but sought clarification on whether the gang management strategy was classified under the Protection of Information Act. If classified, additional processes, including security clearances, would apply; if not, it should be treated like other agenda items. He concluded that, in principle, the presentations justified a closed session.

Mr M Moela (ANC) stated that, given the sensitivity of the matters, the meeting should be closed to ensure Members received all necessary information.

Mr Niehaus noted his general preference for public Committee sessions to maintain transparency and cautioned against routinely closing meetings. However, he supported closing the parts of the meeting dealing with the presentations under the relevant rule.

The Chairperson assured Members that closing the meeting would not become a norm. She explained that the decision was due to the sensitive nature of the information, which could not be shared in an open setting. She emphasised that the meeting needed to be closed so the Committee could be fully briefed on matters that could not be disclosed publicly.

She stated that the gang prevention strategy could not be presented in a live, open session, as doing so could alert gangs to how the Department was combating their operations in correctional facilities. She added that the Committee needed to understand gang operations and the Department’s response, particularly in light of the incident at Pollsmoor Correctional Centre, but such details could not be shared in a normal sitting.

She noted that since the Committee’s inception in June 2024, this was the first time a closed session had been proposed, with all previous meetings held openly to ensure public accountability. She reiterated that it was not intended to become a regular practice.

Before making a final determination, she requested that the Department clarify whether the gang prevention strategy was formally classified under government legislation as top-secret or whether it was treated as confidential due to its sensitive nature. This clarification would guide the Committee’s decision on how to proceed.

Dr Pieter Groenewald, Minister of Correctional Services, stated that he agreed that closed meetings should not become the norm, noting that in his political career he had attended only two closed sessions. He indicated there was a need in this instance and invited the National Commissioner to clarify the classification issue.

He explained that if the document were formally classified, Members would require security clearance, which most did not possess. While he and the Deputy Minister had such clearance, Members of Parliament generally did not. He suggested it was more appropriate to hold a closed session for specific topics rather than formally classifying the document.

He added that gang strategy matters within correctional facilities operated similarly to intelligence functions, involving infiltration and cooperation with security agencies. As such, the information was sensitive and required careful handling. He supported holding a closed session for these discussions.

Mr Samuel Thobakgale, National Commissioner of Correctional Services, stated that the implementation details of the strategy and related interactions required discussion in a closed setting. He also noted implications under POPIA regarding the protection of individuals from the sharing of certain information.

Closing Remarks

The Chairperson stated that Members now had clarity on the nature of the documents. She confirmed that the documents were not formally classified, but their content was sensitive and could not be discussed in an open session, as this would effectively reveal the Department’s prevention strategies against gang activities in correctional facilities.

Based on the information and Members' inputs, she ruled that item four of the agenda would be dealt with in a closed session. She added that once the session concluded, a summary and recommendations would be provided to those requiring a briefing on the outcome.

She then adjourned the open session of the meeting.
