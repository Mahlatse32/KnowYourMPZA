2026 State-of-School Readiness (incl. School Infrastructure, ECD, LTSM, NSNP + Scholar Transport); with Minister

Basic Education

03 February 2026
Chairperson: Ms K Maimela (ANC)
Share this page:

Meeting Summary

Video

The Committee met to consider the state of school readiness, learner admissions, infrastructure, early childhood development (ECD), and learner performance trends. It was noted that concerns were raised about the completeness and credibility of infrastructure condition assessments across provinces, with some assessments still underway. Members expressed unease that incomplete data could undermine the accuracy of information presented to Parliament and complicate constituency engagements where community feedback contradicted official reports. The Department of Basic Education (DBE) indicated that all information presented was sourced from provinces and formally signed off, while acknowledging that verification processes were ongoing.

The meeting highlighted persistent infrastructure challenges, particularly at privately owned ECD centres in rural and under-resourced areas. These challenges included inadequate sanitation, unsafe structures, unreliable access to water, and the continued use of pit latrines. It was reported that private ownership was often cited as a constraint to direct intervention. The Department clarified that its approach to ECD was based on partnership rather than takeover, with registration intended to enable compliance support, access to subsidies, and donor-funded interventions through blended financing models. Members stressed the need for a clearer articulation of roles and responsibilities across national, provincial, and local government, and proposed a dedicated engagement to clarify the ECD delivery model.

Members further raised concerns about declining learner performance in Grade 10, including in historically high-performing schools. It was reported that departmental research confirmed these trends and identified key transition points in the system, notably the shift to English as the language of learning and teaching and the transition into the Further Education and Training (FET) phase. The Department outlined ongoing efforts to strengthen early literacy and numeracy, align assessment standards between primary and secondary schooling, and support smoother transitions through scaffolded teaching and assessment practices.

The issue of learner admissions and placement was also discussed, with particular reference to the use of online and automated systems in certain provinces. It was noted that admissions systems fell within provincial competence and were not uniform nationally. Capacity constraints in high-demand provinces were reported to complicate placement decisions, with the primary objective being to ensure that all learners were placed at the start of the academic year. Members raised concerns about the impact of placement on learner transport, especially where learners were placed far from home, while the Department emphasised that appeals processes were in place and that placements sought to balance proximity, capacity, and availability.

The Committee concluded by adopting its programme and administrative reports, noting amendments to upcoming meetings where necessary. Outstanding information, particularly relating to infrastructure assessments and admissions data, was requested in writing. The Committee emphasised the importance of accurate and timely information for effective oversight and expressed the expectation that future engagements would allow for more in-depth and, where possible, in-person interaction.

Meeting report

Weekly Meetings of the Committee to Ensure Quality Interface and Adequate Response Time

The Chairperson asked the Department of Basic Education (DBE) to leave the meeting for 30 minutes so that the Committee could discuss housekeeping matters.

The Chairperson commended the extensive presentation on the state of school readiness. She highlighted that the report covered issues relating to learner admissions, the delivery of Learning and Teaching Support Material (LTSM), and the readiness of the system to receive learners and commence learning and teaching.

She proposed that the Committee agree to schedule a follow-up meeting with the Provincial Education Department (PED) in Limpopo and the DBE towards the end of March. This was because, during a previous engagement, the Committee had been informed that most of the planned departmental interventions would either be completed or underway by the end of March. This would allow the Committee to consolidate its observations arising from oversight and to engage meaningfully with the PED and DBE on the observations and intervention plans at that stage.

The Chairperson proposed that a special meeting be held at the end of February with the Western Cape and Gauteng provincial departments and the DBE. The purpose of such a meeting would be to closely track progress on the admission challenges being experienced in these provinces. She indicated that these two matters required focused attention and dedicated engagement and proposed that they be addressed through standalone meetings.

She invited Members to engage on the approach to be adopted for the meeting, particularly the style of questioning, noting the need to ensure that all Members were afforded a fair opportunity to engage with the departments during the proceedings.

She asked if the Committee preferred to ask questions and receive an immediate response from the departments. The Committee needed to agree on the number of questions to be allowed, as well as on time allocations for both the departments and the Members of the Committee. She said that Members needed to clarify the intended outcomes of the session.

Members of the Committee were given an opportunity to share their views.

Ms C Jordaan (DA) said that the Committee had not formally adopted its programme, despite proceeding with activities as though the programme had been approved. She suggested that the Committee consider incorporating the formal adoption of the programme into its meetings in the coming weeks. She expressed agreement with the Chairperson’s proposal about engagements with the various departments, noting that such meetings would require dedicated time. She stated that addressing these matters separately would allow the Committee to avoid questioning issues during the current meeting that could be investigated more thoroughly at a later stage. On the style of questioning, she indicated that she did not have a problem with the current approach adopted by the Committee. She stated that the existing practice involved the Department making a presentation, followed by a round of questions from Members. After which, the Department was allowed to respond, with a further round of follow-up questions if necessary. She stated that this was the most appropriate and professional way to conduct engagements on the platform.

She did not support an interrogative style of engagement, explaining that this often resulted in Members entering debate with departments based on their responses. She cautioned that such an approach could limit participation by other Members, as one or two individuals might dominate the discussion.

The Chairperson clarified that the Committee had provisionally adopted the programme. She noted that it had been agreed that the Committee would revisit the programme upon resumption, as Members were at liberty to amend it if necessary. She confirmed that the programme would be accommodated later in the meeting, and requested the Committee Secretary, Mr Llewellyn Brown, to reshare the programme with Members at the end of the session. She indicated that the Committee would then discuss the programme, make any amendments deemed necessary, and submit the amended programme to the Office of the House Chairperson.

She noted Ms Jordaan’s proposal that the Committee maintain its current style of questioning, observing that this approach was constructive and preferable to exchanges that could lead to heightened tensions during engagements with departments. She urged members to submit their proposals on the time allocation and the number of questions a member can ask.

Mr S Louw (ANC) agreed with Ms Jordaan’s view, noting that individual interactive exchanges on the platform were not always effective. He indicated that Members should therefore be afforded allocated time during engagements. He proposed that time be allocated to Members, allowing each Member discretion on how to utilise that time, particularly within the directive that the Committee would focus on the set presentation during the meeting.

Mr Louw supported the proposal that separate meetings be convened to address reports that had highlighted significant issues requiring focused attention. He suggested that the Department be afforded time to make its presentation, after which Members would engage within the allocated time framework. Once a Member’s allocated time had elapsed, the Committee should proceed to the next Member, to ensure orderly participation and to allow the Committee to make progress on matters requiring follow-up.

Mr L Komane (EFF) emphasised the importance of the Committee reaching an agreement on the approach to be followed, to ensure that Members were aligned and shared a common understanding by the end of the process.

He stated that if time were to be allocated, it should be sufficient to allow Members to ask questions in a manner that would leave them satisfied with the engagement. He further highlighted the need to also allocate adequate time to the Departments to respond to questions posed by Members. He cautioned against an approach that resulted in “ticking boxes”, noting that certain questions were of a nature that did not allow the Department officials to provide immediate responses. He expressed concern that insufficient time could lead to inaccurate or incomplete responses being provided to the Committee. Mr Komane proposed that questions be grouped, and that Departments be allowed to provide written responses where information was not readily available. He said that this approach would enable more accurate and meaningful engagement. He urged the Chairperson to take Members’ concerns into consideration.

Chairperson’s opening remarks

The Chairperson indicated that the Department had been requested to rejoin the meeting at 10:00 and asked Mr Brown to confirm that the Department had reconnected.

She apologised to the Department for the 30 minutes delay, explaining that this was the first meeting of the Portfolio Committee for the year and that several housekeeping matters had required attention.

She formally welcomed Members of the Portfolio Committee, the Minister, the Deputy Minister, officials from the Department, and all stakeholders in attendance. She extended warm wishes for the year ahead, noting that this was the Committee’s first official meeting of the year.

She said that the Committee wished to convey its heartfelt condolences to the families, friends, schools, and the Gauteng Department of Education (GDE) following the tragic loss of approximately 12 learners who died in a road accident in Vanderbijlpark during the first week of the schools' reopening. She stated that the incident had sent shockwaves across the country and had deeply affected South Africans.

She expressed the Committee’s profound sadness at the loss of young lives, noting that lives could have been saved if it were not for reckless driving. She acknowledged the pain experienced by the parents. She said that the accident had highlighted the vulnerability of learners commuting to and from schools. The Chairperson welcomed the President’s call for relevant departments to convene and scrutinise the learner and scholar transport system, with a view to developing recommendations. She indicated that the Committee looked forward to engaging with the outcomes of the interministerial process once its work had been concluded.

She also took the opportunity to congratulate the Class of 2025 on their achievements, noting their improved performance and successful completion of the academic year. She further congratulated teachers, school governing bodies, and the Department, including provincial offices, districts, and circuits, for the support provided to learners. She acknowledged the extensive effort observed during oversight visits, noting that teachers frequently worked beyond official hours and went above and beyond the call of duty to ensure learners were adequately prepared for examinations. While recognising the contribution of educators and the Department, she emphasised that the primary credit belonged to the learners themselves. She further congratulated the Department on achieving an overall pass rate of 88%.

The Chairperson noted the Department’s emphasis on the quality of passes and indicated that this was an area the Committee wished to engage in further. She cautioned against equating quality outcomes solely with bachelor's-level passes. She stressed the importance of recognising passes that enable learners to access Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) colleges as equally valuable. She emphasised the need to shift the language and culture around educational success, noting that technical skills were as important as professional skills and that current discourse could inadvertently discourage learners who did not gain university admission. She expressed concern about the decline in performance in Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEM) subjects, particularly the limited improvement in the number of learners achieving passes in these areas. She noted that, during back-to-school campaigns conducted by Members in their constituencies, some school principals had attributed lower overall school performance to poor results in STEM subjects. This was a matter of concern for the Committee. She also welcomed the Department’s reported successes in Quintile 1 to Quintile 3 schools and expressed optimism about the quality of performance in these schools. She stated that with adequate and targeted support, these schools demonstrated significant potential for excellence. The Chairperson called for sustained focus and investment to unlock this potential.

She further acknowledged the impact of teacher development programmes, noting that such results would not be possible without continuous professional development for educators. She affirmed the Committee’s support for initiatives and resources allocated to teacher development, describing it as a cornerstone for improving learner performance. The Chairperson also supported the Minister’s view about the contribution of broader government interventions, including social protection measures, the NSNP, learner transport initiatives, and the provision of fully subsidised schools in rural areas. She noted that these interventions played a critical role in learner retention and in achieving the unprecedented number of learners remaining in school and successfully completing matric.

She expressed concern about the transition of learners from basic education to higher education, noting existing challenges within the education sector. She proposed that the Portfolio Committee consider convening a joint meeting with the Portfolio Committee on Higher Education to enable both departments to engage on strategies to improve the transition of learners from basic education into post-school education and training.

The Chairperson stated that the Portfolio Committee had commenced the year with an oversight visit to the province of Limpopo, during which several challenges had been observed. She noted that some of these challenges were extreme and urgent, particularly those relating to the state of school infrastructure. She indicated that the Committee had agreed to schedule a follow-up meeting by the end of March with both the PED in Limpopo and the DBE, to engage comprehensively on the Committee’s observations and on the intervention plans that the Department had committed to implementing. She explained that, because of this decision, the Committee would not use the current meeting to deliberate on the oversight observations. Instead, a dedicated meeting would be convened to focus specifically on the observations and the proposed interventions, to ensure a more effective engagement.

The Chairperson raised concerns about the admissions and placement challenges for Grade 1 and Grade 8 learners, particularly in Gauteng and the Western Cape. She noted that media reports and social media platforms had highlighted instances of parents queuing at schools and expressing frustration, including perceptions that undocumented foreign national learners were being prioritised over South African learners. She described this as a difficult and sensitive narrative, noting the visible frustration experienced by parents in the affected provinces. She expressed serious concern about the number of learners who remained unplaced, indicating that more than 2 500 learners in Gauteng and over 6 000 learners in the Western Cape had not yet been accommodated. She described this as a crisis and stated that, while the issue formed part of the Department’s presentation and would be engaged during the meeting, the Committee intended to convene a dedicated follow-up meeting at the end of February with the affected provinces. The purpose of this engagement would be to assess progress and to discuss long-term solutions to prevent the recurrence of admissions crises in these provinces.

The Chairperson indicated that the Committee would engage with the Department’s report on the state of school readiness for 2026. She highlighted issues raised by parents, including shortages of stationery, which had resulted in schools, particularly no-fee schools, requesting parents to cover shortfalls. She further noted concerns relating to overcrowding in schools, teacher development, and learner performance beyond Grade 12. She also raised the issue of budget reallocations within the Department, noting that some complaints received from districts and parents suggested that national budget reallocations had negatively affected provincial operations. She indicated that the Committee wished to hear the Department’s perspective on these matters, as they formed part of the concerns raised during the reopening of schools and had already been referred to the Department for response.

She expressed the Committee’s expectation that the engagement with the Department would be effective and constructive. She apologised to Members for not activating her camera, citing unstable network connectivity, and indicated that doing so would further disrupt the meeting.

The Chairperson thereafter invited Mr Brown to indicate any apologies received and to present the agenda for adoption, after which the Committee would proceed to the scheduled items.

Mr Brown indicated that Ms L Ngobeni (Action SA) was on the platform but had requested to be excused due to travel commitments. He recorded apologies from Ms Y Govana (ANC), who was on oversight, Ms T Magagula (ANC), Ms P Mkhize (MKP), Mr S Mkhize (MKP), and Mr M Shikwambana (EFF).

Mr Brown thereafter indicated that he would proceed to present the agenda for the meeting.

The Chairperson thanked Mr Brown and noted the apologies from Members of the Select Committee of the National Council of Provinces, who had been invited to the meeting. She indicated that the Committee was aware that the Select Committee was currently conducting an oversight visit in Limpopo and extended well wishes for the successful completion of their oversight programme.

The Chairperson indicated that the Minister would proceed to introduce the Departmental delegation. She stated that the Committee would receive a presentation on the state of school readiness. She indicated that the Committee would consider outstanding ministerial matters and the draft report on the release of the 2025 National Senior Certificate results.

She welcomed the Minister, the Deputy Minister, and the Director-General (DG), and invited the Minister to proceed with the presentation.

Minister’s remarks

Ms Siviwe Gwarube, Minister of Basic Education, greeted the Chairperson and Members of the Portfolio Committee and welcomed the opportunity to engage with the Committee at the start of the parliamentary year. She expressed appreciation for the continued collaboration between the Department and Parliament as both parties fulfil their respective accountability roles.

She informed the Committee that she was joined by the Deputy Minister, the DG, and senior departmental officials. The Minister indicated that the Department was appearing before the Committee to account for progress made in preparing for the 2026 academic year. Since the second half of the previous year and particularly during the final quarter of the 2025 academic year, the Department had been closely monitoring the state of readiness for 2026. This monitoring process included regular engagements with the DG, ongoing communication with Members of Executive Councils (MECs) responsible for education, and direct oversight of emerging risks. These risks were related to learner admissions and placement, infrastructure readiness, learner transport, staffing, the availability of learning and teaching support materials, and learner support services.

Since the Committee’s previous engagement with the Department, the National Senior Certificate (NSC) examination results had been released, and the Department had concluded its Lekgotla, which was attended by some Committee Members. These processes had been instrumental in sharpening the Department’s focus on foundational learning, system stability, and operational readiness at the start of the academic year.

She concurred with the Chairperson's remarks in conveying condolences to the families and communities of learners who had lost their lives in Gauteng. On behalf of the DBE, she extended sincere condolences to the affected families and school communities. She described the incident as a devastating loss. She emphasised that it underscored the importance of learner safety, including the provision of safe and reliable learner transport across all spheres of government. While investigations were being addressed through appropriate processes, the tragedy highlighted the significance of the oversight engagement before the Committee.

The Minister clarified that the presentation before the Committee constituted a progress report rather than a declaration of perfection. The information reflected submissions by provincial education departments at a particular point in time. She cautioned that readiness was not static. Learner admissions, placement, staffing allocations, and support services continued to evolve as provinces responded to enrolment pressures and localised challenges.

She stressed that readiness should be understood comprehensively, extending beyond learner placement figures to include conditions for effective teaching and learning. These conditions included the availability of educators, learning and teaching support materials, school nutrition, learner transport, safe and adequate infrastructure, and appropriate support for Grade R and early learning. While national learner placement figures were encouraging, he cautioned that percentages alone did not reflect the full picture. The Minister emphasised the need for vigilance in identified pressure areas, particularly high-growth urban provinces such as Gauteng and the Western Cape, as well as historically under-resourced communities where challenges could arise rapidly. In this regard, she requested ongoing verification and refinement of provincial data, as well as formal sign-off by Heads of Department (HOD) where required.

She reiterated that provinces remained the primary sites of implementation and reported that she had engaged with MECs to ensure that readiness interventions were actively managed at provincial and district levels. She informed the Committee that she had convened a meeting of the Council of Education Ministers to specifically address the state of readiness for the 2026 academic year and to consider interventions aimed at mitigating emerging risks.

The Minister indicated that challenges relating to learner transport, infrastructure backlogs, staffing pressures, and delays in the delivery of learning materials were being escalated and addressed through appropriate intergovernmental processes. She acknowledged the Committee’s oversight role as central to strengthening accountability within the education system. She said that the DBE welcomed the robust engagement from Members and expressed confidence that the discussion would assist in identifying early risks, closing remaining gaps, and ensuring that no learner was left behind at the start of the 2026 academic year. She reaffirmed the Department’s commitment to working constructively with Parliament, provincial governments, and education stakeholders to stabilise the system and deliver safe, quality basic education.

The Minister invited the Deputy Director-General (DDG) of the DBE, Ms Simone Geyer, to present.

Presentation by the DBE Progress report on the 2026 school year readiness

Ms Geyer took the Committee through the progress report on the 2026 school year readiness.

To present a progress report on the 2026 School Year readiness on seven key issues:

  • Learner placement
  • Provision of LTSM
  • Post provisioning
  • Learner transport
  • Early Childhood Development
  • School infrastructure
  • National School Nutrition Programme

Learner placement

  • Learner placement data is dynamic; it changes every day
  • At the time when this presentation was prepared, the 10-day statistics were not available
  • Learner placement statistics presented in this presentation are up to 28 January 2026
  • PEDs have ensured that all learners who applied are matched with a school, and all special cases are being considered. These include:
    • Making provisions in place to accommodate late applications due to practical reasonable parents matters
    • Putting in place designated walk-in centres
    • Allocating mobile classes in schools in the hotspot areas to ensure that all learners are accommodated. For example, NC has provided 78 mobile classrooms in 37 schools.

Provision of LTSM

As of 28 January 2026:

  • All provinces (except KZN) have ordered all the textbooks required to top up those that were procured in previous years
  • In EC, FS, GP and WC the ordered textbooks have been delivered to all schools, which made orders
  • MP has delivered the orders in warehouses
  • KZN did not make any orders, while NC made a few orders

Post provision

  • The information on additional teachers due to increased enrolments becomes available shortly after the 10th official school day, once schools’ enrolment data has been captured, verified, and approved
  • Provincial Education Departments (PEDs) set aside ad hoc posts to ensure the education system remains responsive, equitable, and stable despite enrolment changes after the start of the school year

Learner Transport

  • As at 28 January 2026:
  • Nationally, 64% of the learners who need transport are not getting it
  • Provinces with a larger number of learners who not transported are GP and NC
  • WC is the only province, which reported that all learners who need transport are transported

Discussion

The Chairperson welcomed the presentation and invited Members to engage with the presentation.

Mr Louw welcomed the presentation and noted that it was important to acknowledge both the progress made and the significant challenges that remained. He indicated that he had reviewed the presentation in advance and wished to raise several concerns arising from persistent and recurring challenges confronting the Portfolio Committee.

He drew attention to regional disparities across provinces, noting that some provinces continued to perform at lower levels, particularly across certain grades. He highlighted uneven progress in the delivery of LTSM, citing delays in textbook deliveries. He referred to the Eastern Cape, where Grade 1 and Grade 7 coverage stood at 13.5%.

The presentation reflected the vulnerability of supply chains to environmental shocks, including floods and adverse weather conditions, and stressed the need for more robust contingency planning. Mr Louw argued that disruptions in the delivery of educational materials constituted a direct disruption to learning and teaching.

He expressed concern with educator vacancies, with specific reference to the Northern Cape, where the vacancy rate stood at 14.6%.

He asked how the DBE defined school readiness beyond compliance-based indicators. What measures were in place to ensure the quality of teaching and learning from the first day of the school year? He enquired about the mechanisms the Department employed to identify provinces that were at risk or underperforming prior to the commencement of the academic year.

What factors contributed to lower learner placement percentages in certain provinces, particularly Grade R in the Western Cape? What mechanisms were in place to monitor the educational impact of learner placement? How had the Department intervened in provinces that failed to achieve universal textbook coverage, especially where this affected foundational phase learners?

He requested information on the contingency plans in place to mitigate disruptions caused by natural disasters, such as floods experienced in Mpumalanga and other provinces.

He noted that KZN transported only 39% of learners in need, despite exceeding provincial targets. He asked how the Department ensured the adequacy of learner transport needs assessments.

Mr Louw noted that approximately 10% of schools remained in poor or very poor condition and asked what the realistic timeline was for eliminating unsafe school infrastructure. Why had only 76% of the Education Infrastructure Grant (EIG) been spent by mid-January 2026? What consequences existed for provinces that consistently underspent allocated funds?

He stated that he received an allegation about a post within the Department’s communications unit. He reported allegations of undue interference by individuals from the Ministerial Office. The individuals were alleged to have approached the Human Resources unit to unlawfully introduce curriculum vitae that had not been submitted through the prescribed application process, with the intention of influencing appointments to Information and Communications Technology (ICT) related posts. This, he said, raised serious concerns about the separation of political and administrative authority.

He requested that the DG investigate these allegations and provide a report on whether such allegations had substance, including whether any curriculum vitae were improperly introduced into the recruitment process, potentially disadvantageous to applicants who had followed the correct procedures. He reiterated his concern about the overall state of readiness.

Ms Jordaan greeted the Committee and the Department and conveyed compliments of the new year. She acknowledged the extensive work undertaken by schools, educators, and administrators to ensure readiness for the reopening of schools, as reflected in the presentation. She echoed the sentiments of her colleagues in extending sincere condolences to families and school communities that had experienced tragic losses in recent weeks. She made a reference to the twelve learners who lost their lives in a tragic accident, and a Grade 11 learner who was fatally stabbed at MD Moetanalo Secondary School in Mpumalanga. These incidents underscored persistent concerns about learner safety, both during travel to and from school and within school premises. She noted recent media reports highlighting aggressive behaviour and safety challenges in schools.

Ms Jordaan congratulated the Class of 2025 on their matric results, acknowledging the perseverance of learners, educators, and school leadership, and described their achievement as a source of national pride.

She welcomed the emphasis placed on ECD in the presentation, noting its importance as a foundation for lifelong learning. She emphasised the need for continued collaboration between the Department, provinces, local government, civil society, and the private sector to strengthen ECD centres, improve operational systems, and ensure that all children have access to quality early learning opportunities. She stressed the importance of supporting ECD centres to achieve appropriate registration and compliance levels.

She said that she would not raise extensive questions on learner placement, as the Committee had agreed to engage separately with provinces experiencing particular pressures. However, she sought clarity on learner placement practices in Gauteng, recalling that learners were initially placed according to parental preference, with automatic placement at alternative schools where congestion occurred. She asked if this recollection was correct and how such practices affected learner transport costs, particularly considering reports that approximately 10 000 learners in Gauteng were not receiving learner transport. She asked if similar placement practices were implemented in other provinces.

While textbooks and stationery had largely been completed, LTSM delivery figures for KZN and the Northern Cape remained significantly low. She observed that budget constraints had been cited as a reason and expressed concern that schools experiencing delays in textbook delivery were also struggling with stationery and workbook provision. She asked if this meant that learners in provinces reflecting zero delivery figures were currently without essential learning materials. What mitigation measures were in place to address the situation?

She noted that the presentation outlined mitigation measures for vacancies in the Western Cape and KZN but did not reflect similar measures for Gauteng. She asked if Gauteng had a specific strategy in place to address post-provisioning challenges in the province.

She reiterated concerns about learner safety and asked how many schools currently failed to meet minimum safety standards. Written information on this matter would be accepted should the data be too extensive.

She noted that the presentation reflected the number of schools participating in the NSNP but did not clearly indicate the total number of schools that should be participating per province. She requested clarity on provincial coverage levels, specifically if the reported figures represented full coverage according to departmental targets. She asked that participation be reflected as a percentage per province.

She stated that while inputs into the education system were important, outcomes were critical in assessing whether the system was delivering for learners. She asked if the Department tracked literacy levels of Grade 3 and Grade 4 learners at the beginning of the school year and measured progress against end-of-year outcomes to assess reading for meaning. This also applied to Grade 6 learners and minimum mathematics competency.

Mr Komane greeted the Chairperson, the Committee, and the Department and apologised for his background noise as he was at the airport.

He noted that despite the Department's repeated reporting on interventions, there appeared to be little substantive progress in resolving longstanding challenges. The education system seemed to be experiencing the same difficulties year after year. He said that presentations often reflected positive compliance on paper, but this did not always translate into meaningful improvement in practice.

He highlighted ongoing challenges in learner admissions and placement, noting that learners who had applied timeously, as early as November of the previous year, remained unplaced when schools had already reopened. As an example, Mr Komane cited a case in Potchefstroom, reference number 1211, involving Ferdinand Postma High School. A parent had applied the previous year, but the learner had still not been placed. Why did learners who had applied late appear to be considered ahead of those who had complied with application timelines? What factors were contributing to this situation?

There was another case involving a warrant officer employed by the South African Police Service (SAPS), who had applied for learner placement at Eden Gate High School, Fairmont High School, and Tygerberg High School in the Western Cape. Due to the nature of her work, the officer had requested placement closer to her place of deployment, and the SAPS had provided a recommendation in support of the application. Despite this, the learner had not been placed. He expressed concern that if an official serving in a critical public safety role could experience such difficulties, ordinary parents might face even greater challenges. This matter had been referred to Mr Mahata (no record from the DBE) for further consideration. The Minister confirmed that funding support for ECD centres included donor and philanthropic funding, including projects supported through initiatives such as Float, as well as the newly established Education Outcomes Fund (EOF). She explained that this fund operated through a blended financing model, whereby government funding was matched by private-sector contributions, enabling scalable support for compliance and quality improvement across the sector.

The Minister acknowledged concerns raised by Members about Grade 10 performance declines. She confirmed that departmental research aligned with observations made during oversight visits. She identified two key inflexion points in the education system. The first point was at Grade 4, where learners transitioned to English as the language of learning and teaching. The second point was within the FET phase, particularly between Grades 10 and 12.

The Minister noted that weak foundational learning exacerbated later learning difficulties, which was why the Department placed strong emphasis on strengthening early literacy and numeracy. She indicated that increased repetition rates, reduced participation in mathematics and science, and transition challenges in the FET phase were currently under review by the Department’s research teams, with targeted interventions under development.

In response to the issue of data credibility, the Minister stated that all information presented to the Committee was sourced directly from provinces and formally signed off by HODs and MECs. She emphasised that the Department operated on a principle of institutional trust, while acknowledging that physical verification and monitoring took time and were conducted on an ongoing basis. She confirmed that the data presented was current and had been received from provinces within days of the meeting.

The Deputy Minister indicated that provinces determined their own admission requirements. There was no nationally uniform admissions system. She noted that, to her knowledge, only Gauteng and the Western Cape currently utilised online admissions systems, and that these systems operated strictly within provincial competence.

The Deputy Minister highlighted that provinces such as Gauteng faced significant capacity constraints, making it increasingly difficult to accommodate all parental preferences. In this context, the primary provincial priority was to ensure that every learner was placed in a school, particularly once the ten-day enrolment statistics were finalised. The overriding objective was to avoid learners remaining unplaced at the start of the academic year.

She acknowledged concerns raised by Members about the impact of placement decisions on learner transport, especially where learners were placed in schools that were not geographically closest to their homes. However, she clarified that placements were not made arbitrarily or through automatic relegation. Provinces continued to consider appeals processes, and efforts were made to balance proximity, capacity, and availability.

The Deputy Minister cited the Western Cape MEC’s assertion that each appeal was individually reviewed to determine the most appropriate placement for learners. Nonetheless, she emphasised that once schools reached maximum capacity, the province’s immediate responsibility was to ensure that learners were placed somewhere within the system so that learning and teaching could commence without further delay.

DG Mweli said that the Minister was correct that there was no online admissions system implemented nationally. This was particularly relevant for rural and remote areas, where access to such facilities was limited or non-existent. At present, the system was primarily in use in Gauteng, with some provinces, such as the Western Cape, having piloted online admissions for specific grades. However, online admission was not prevalent across the country.

He admitted that learner transport placed a significant burden on parents when their children were not placed in schools within walking distance. Parents then bore the cost of transporting learners to schools further away. Gauteng, in particular, faced unique challenges in demand and placement, as highlighted by the Minister.

The data for the textbooks and learners affected would be sourced and made available to the Committee.

He said that the Minister had extensively addressed the transition between Grades 10 and 12. However, earlier discussion had focused on curriculum provisioning and assessment, particularly in Grades 8 and, to some extent, Grade 9. These grades reflected learners’ readiness to receive teaching and demonstrate knowledge, with Grade 10 marking the stage where many learners experienced performance losses.

The Department's plan to address this involved scaffolding assessment from primary school. Since the discontinuation of the Annual National Assessment (ANA), it has become evident that assessment standards in primary schools are insufficiently aligned with high school demands, causing learners to struggle when they transition to high school.

Over the past three years, the Department has required subject advisors in primary schools (senior phase) to collaborate with high school subject advisors (FET phase). This collaboration ensured that standards were scaffolded, supporting a smooth transition from Grade 7 to Grades 8 and 9 in high school, and ultimately preparing learners for the FET phase, where subject choices were made in Grade 10.

In essence, this approach addressed two objectives, which included maintaining learner retention within the system to prevent learners from getting lost, and ensuring smooth progression in teaching and learning across grades, from Grade 7 through to Grade 10 and beyond, using scaffolding techniques that teachers were trained to implement.

The Chairperson noticed a message from Mr S Mkhize (MKP), who had submitted an apology. She did not allow Mr Mkhize to contribute on the grounds that the Committee had agreed on an approach for the meeting. Any follow-up interventions had to align with the agreed approach.

Mr Mkhize was instructed to submit his contributions in writing to the Committee secretary, who would forward them to the Department for consideration and intervention.

The Chairperson thanked the Minister, the Deputy Minister, the DG, and the departmental delegation for the opportunity to engage on the state of school readiness.

She indicated that she had expected a response from Mr Mafoko on the number of provinces that had completed the conditional assessment of school readiness. She requested that, should the Department not be in a position to respond verbally, this information be included in the comprehensive written responses to be submitted to the Committee. She emphasised that this information was critical in determining whether the reported state of school infrastructure accurately reflected conditions on the ground.

She stressed that Members of the Committee relied on information provided by the Department when engaging with communities during constituency work. She cautioned that if departmental information could not be confirmed as accurate, it placed Members in a difficult position when community feedback contradicted official reports. She therefore reiterated the importance of clarity on how many provinces had concluded the conditional assessment, particularly in relation to infrastructure readiness.

She proposed that the Committee convene a dedicated engagement on ECDs, noting that a similar engagement had taken place in the previous year. She recalled that, following that engagement, the Committee had requested clarity on the Department’s ECD delivery model. She explained that this request was informed by the President’s stated priority to expand access to early childhood learning and to strengthen foundational learning as a basis for improved academic performance in later years.

While the government had committed to delivering ECD services in partnership with non-governmental organisations, there remained a lack of clarity about roles and responsibilities. She highlighted concerns raised during oversight visits, where ECD practitioners and centre owners had reported inadequate support, particularly in relation to infrastructure, and were often told that such facilities were privately owned.

She argued that a comprehensive discussion was required to clearly outline the ECD delivery model, including roles at national, provincial, and local government levels. She observed that there appeared to be a disjuncture between the understanding of the Department at head office and that of provincial administrations, as well as uncertainty about the role of local government, which had constitutionally assigned responsibilities, including in relation to infrastructure and related services.

She requested that the outstanding responses be submitted to the Committee in writing. She thanked the Minister, the Deputy Minister, and the Department for their engagement. She indicated that the delegation would be released to allow the Committee to proceed with its internal deliberations on outstanding agenda items.

She expressed the hope that future engagements would take place in person to facilitate more effective interaction.

Report of the Portfolio Committee on Basic Education on the Official Release of the National Senior Certificate (NSC) Results for 2025

The Chairperson indicated that the report before the Committee related to its attendance at the 2025 NSC results release. She noted that the report had been circulated to Members some time earlier.

She expressed the expectation that Members would have had an opportunity to consider the contents of the report. She therefore invited Members to indicate whether there were any proposed corrections or amendments to the report.

Members did not comment. The Chairperson therefore asked Members to adopt the report as it was.

Consideration of Committee Minutes

The Chairperson presented the minutes of the meeting held on 2 December 2025, being the last meeting at which the Committee had received a briefing on the status of care.

The Chairperson therefore invited Members to indicate whether there were any corrections or amendments to be made. Members did not respond. The Chairperson therefore asked Members to adopt the minutes as they were.

Mr Louw said that he had gone through the minutes and accepted them as a true reflection of the meeting held on 2 December 2025. He moved for the adoption of the report. Ms Xaba-Ntshaba seconded this motion.

Programming and related matters

The Chairperson asked the Committee Secretary to present the Committee programme for the 1st term of 2026. She asked Members if there were any amendments they wished to make to the programme.

She proposed that a meeting be scheduled on a Friday towards the end of February with Gauteng Province and the Western Cape Government to receive an updated report on learner admissions. A second engagement was proposed for the end of March with the DBE and the Limpopo Provincial Education Department to receive a progress update on the Committee’s oversight observations.

She requested Mr Brown to check the parliamentary schedule and programme, particularly given that Fridays were often reserved for plenary sittings. The purpose was to determine how best these two meetings could be accommodated within the parliamentary programme.

As an alternative arrangement, she suggested that the meeting scheduled for 24 February be adjusted by replacing the North West Province with the Western Cape. This adjustment would allow the Committee to avoid convening a meeting on a Friday. Further, the scope of that meeting could be revised by reducing the focus on bullying incidents and anti-bullying policies and instead incorporating an update on learner admissions. This update could be included as part of the existing presentation without difficulty.

This approach might assist the Committee in accommodating the required updates within the current programme while minimising scheduling constraints.

She asked Members if they had any proposals. She asked Ms Jordaan if she had amendments she wanted to make. Ms Jordaan did not respond.

The Chairperson stated that the programme had been adopted.

Mr Brown informed the Committee that the meeting scheduled for Tuesday, 10 February, would need to be removed from the programme. He explained that a Members’ training session had been scheduled for the same date, which would make it impossible for the Committee to convene.

He proposed that the engagement with the Department of Higher Education be rescheduled to an alternative date, which would still fall within the current parliamentary term.

The Chairperson accepted this proposal and requested confirmation about the proposed meeting date at the end of March. She asked that guidance be provided once the date had been assessed.

Mr Brown responded that he would review the matter. He stated that the parliamentary programme would be consulted, including the scheduling of possible plenaries or mini plenaries. He confirmed that efforts would be made to utilise the last Friday in March.

Mr Louw indicated that the programme had been circulated previously and noted that it had been well aligned with the Committee’s ongoing processes. He proposed that, if Members were content, the programme be re-adopted.

The Chairperson stated that the programme was in order and requested that, after Mr Brown had made the amendment, it be submitted to the House Chairperson.

The Chairperson thanked Members and officials for their time and for a constructive meeting.

The meeting was adjourned.
